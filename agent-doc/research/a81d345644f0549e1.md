### A.2 PHC's actual mechanisms (I pulled these from the paper, not from memory)

- **Hard-negative mining over motions.** Evaluate the converged policy over the whole dataset; sequences it fails become `Q̂_hard^(1)`. Train the next primitive *only* on those. Nested: `Q̂_hard^(k) ⊆ Q̂_hard^(k−1)`. Convergence = "success rate on `Q̂_hard^(k)` no longer increases." The repo exposes this as `scripts/pmcp/forward_pmcp.py`, which dumps the failure set.
- **PMCP composer:** `π_PHC(a|s) = (1/C(s)) ∏_i P^(i)(a^(i)|s)^{C(s)}` over frozen primitives plus a fail-recovery primitive `P^(F)`. Multiplicative, all primitives active simultaneously.
- **Fail-state recovery:** triggered by fallen / >0.5 m from reference / both. During recovery all joint rotations except root are replaced by simulated values in the goal state; goal switches back to full imitation at <0.5 m.
- **Relaxed Early Termination (RET):** excludes **ankle and toe joints** from the 0.5 m distance check, so foot-slide retargeting artifacts don't kill otherwise-good episodes. **Your analogue: exclude finger joints entirely from termination.**
- **Reward:** `r = 0.5·r^g + 0.5·r^amp + r^energy`, with `exp(−100‖p̂−p‖)` position, `exp(−10‖q̂⊖q‖)` rotation, `exp(−0.1‖·‖)` velocities; energy `−0.0005·Σ|μ_j·ω_j|²`.
- **No reference-noise injection during training.** Its robustness comes from (a) removing reference dependence from the action space, (b) fail-recovery, (c) training on *filtered clean* AMASS. Result: ~90% success on video-estimated poses vs 18–58% for baselines.

**UHC** (Universal Humanoid Controller) is the KinPoly/EmbodiedPose-branch predecessor: [ZhengyiLuo/UHC](https://github.com/ZhengyiLuo/UHC). Its own README says it "relies heavily on residual force control for keeping the humanoid stable, and we are actively working on relying less on this dependency."

### A.3 Soft / adaptive tracking tolerance — this is where the real wins are

**KungfuBot / PBHC** — [2506.12851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12851) · [TeleHuman/PBHC](https://github.com/TeleHuman/PBHC) · NeurIPS 2025. The only paper with a *derived* answer for the exponential reward's σ. Appendix A shows the optimal tracking factor is the mean optimal tracking error, `σ* = (Σ x_i*)/N`, which is circular, so they close the loop online:

```
x̂ ← EMA(instantaneous tracking error)
σ ← min(σ, x̂)                     # monotonically non-increasing, init large
```

Their §5.4 ablation is exactly the reward-noise-sensitivity study you asked for: four fixed-σ configs (Coarse / Medium / UpperBound / LowerBound) each win on *some* motions and lose on others; adaptive σ is near-optimal on all of them. The paper's framing — "no single fixed tracking factor works across motions of varying quality and complexity" — is your argument for why you need this under three noise tiers.

Their motion filter, also useful: CoM–CoP distance `Δd_t = ‖p̄^CoM − p̄^CoP‖₂ < ε_stab`, **ε_stab = 0.1 m**; rejected motions max out at **54% episode-length ratio**, accepted ones are consistently high.

**AdaMimic** — [2510.14454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14454) · [InternRobotics/AdaMimic](https://github.com/InternRobotics/AdaMimic) · G1. Sparsifies a single reference into keyframes, trains a policy to generate dense intermediates, then trains adapters that **adjust tracking speed** — flexible time warping. Relevant because monocular reconstruction gets timing wrong, not just geometry.

**ConTrack** — [2606.03177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03177) · [lyt0112.com/projects/ConTrack](https://www.lyt0112.com/projects/ConTrack) · no code. **The single most on-point paper for your problem statement.** It treats object tracking as a *constraint* and spends leftover control authority on motion fidelity, with an online dual update:

```
J_g ← running mean of discounted episodic return from the object reward
J_g* ← running max
λ   ← λ + η(α − Ĵ_g / J_g*)          # α = target ratio
w_task ← σ(λ)
A_mix ← w_task·A_g + (1 − w_task)·A_s + A_p     # mixes ADVANTAGES, not rewards
```

Ablations (Table 4, 5, 6):

| Variant | Progress ↑ | Obj pos (m) ↓ | Contact F1 ↑ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adaptive mixing | **0.899** | **0.026** | **0.784** |
| Fixed task weight | 0.764 | 0.026 | 0.679 |
| Fixed 1:1 | 0.868 | 0.029 | 0.701 |
| Reset-from-start | 0.700 | 0.039 | 0.739 |
| Uniform mid-clip reset | 0.727 | 0.033 | 0.714 |
| w/o contact reward | 0.861 | 0.032 | 0.699 |

Sweeping α traces a Pareto frontier; every fixed-mixing policy is dominated. Their framing of the transformation — "a framework that transforms physically-infeasible reference motion into executable robotic trajectories" — is yours.

**SoftMimic** — [2510.17792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17792) · [Improbable-AI/softmimic](https://github.com/Improbable-AI/softmimic) · G1. The architectural pattern here is worth more than the compliance result: the policy **observes `q_ref`** (the nominal/noisy reference) **but is rewarded for matching `q_aug`** (the physically-correct augmented target). You have exactly this asymmetry available for free — at training time you hold tier-1 GT, so train with tier-2/3 as observation and tier-1 as the reward target.

**Constrained Style Learning from Imperfect Demonstrations under Task Optimality** — [2507.09371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09371) · CoRL 2025 · Hutter group. CMDP with an adaptively-adjusted Lagrange multiplier that suppresses the imitation term whenever it costs task performance. Same idea as ConTrack, legged-robot flavor, hardware-validated on ANYmal-D.

**PGMT** — [2609.08511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08511) — "terrain-aware tracking relaxation allows necessary deviations from the reference while preserving its motion intent."

## B. Reward shaping that degrades gracefully

### B.1 The reward that matches your metric

Your score is ADD-AUC. **ResMimic's point-cloud object reward is literally ADD inside an exponential:**

```
r^o_t = exp(−λ_o · Σ_{i=1}^N ‖P[i]_t − P̂[i]_t‖₂)     # P = N points sampled from object mesh
```

vs the conventional `exp(−λ_p‖p−p̂‖) + w·exp(−λ_θ‖θ⊖θ̂‖)`. Their stated reason: **smoother reward landscape, and it "naturally accounts for both translation and rotation, eliminating the need for task-specific weight tuning."** You are being scored on a point-set distance; reward a point-set distance. Same for termination: `‖P_t − P̂_t‖₂ > σ_o`.

**Canonical exponential form and σ.** BeyondMimic: `r(ē_s, σ_s) = exp(−ē_s/σ_s²)` summed over `{p, R, v, ω}`, "with nominal error σ_s determined empirically." Shipped values from its Isaac Lab config: body pos 0.3, body ori 0.4, lin vel 1.0, ang vel 3.14.

### B.2 Object-centric vs imitation-level — why object wins under noisy hand refs

This is the best-supported claim in the whole report. Five independent lines of evidence:

1. **DexMan** ([2510.08475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08475) · [EmbodiedAI-NTU/DexMan](https://github.com/EmbodiedAI-NTU/DexMan)) names the dilemma precisely: *"when reference motions are noisy or physically implausible, as in single-view video estimates, strict imitation can push policies toward infeasible behaviors"* — but relaxed imitation collapses into a contact-avoiding local minimum. Their fix shifts *"the learning objective away from imitating ineffective hand trajectories towards fulfilling object-centric contact goals."*

2. **WristMimic** (ECCV 2026, [2607.06438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06438)) is the strongest form: the body and **wrist** follow kinematic targets; **fingers get no kinematic supervision at all** — they learn from object tracking and contact outcomes. Joint reward excludes all 30 finger joints: `r^α_t = exp(−λ^α Σ_{j∈J_cf} w_j(t)E_j^α)`, and inside a contact window `[t_c − t_b, t_c + t_a]` the weights are **0 for arm joints, 1 for wrist, w_red < 1 for everything else**. They match or surpass methods using full finger-pose supervision, and gain cross-embodiment transfer for free. For G1+Dex3 this is the design.

3. **Human2Sim2Robot** ([2504.12609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12609) · [tylerlum/human2sim2robot](https://github.com/tylerlum/human2sim2robot)): from one RGB-D video, extract exactly two things — object pose trajectory (→ dense reward) and pre-manipulation hand pose (→ initialization only, never a tracking target). **+55% over object-aware replay, +68% over IL.**

4. **DexTrack** ([2502.09614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09614) · [Meowuu7/DexTrack](https://github.com/Meowuu7/DexTrack), ICLR 2025) reward weights: `w_obj,pos = 1.0`, `w_obj,rot = 0.3`, `w_hand,wrist = 0.1`, `w_hand,finger = 0.1`. Object position gets **10×** the hand terms.

5. **HuDOR** ([2410.23289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23289) · [object-rewards.github.io](https://object-rewards.github.io/)) computes rewards from object-oriented point-tracker trajectories in human video, deliberately bypassing the human↔robot hand morphology gap. **4× over baselines.** Also **Robot See Robot Do** ([2409.18121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18121), CoRL 2024): *"rather than mimicking hand movements, the robot plans bimanual arm motions designed to recreate the same object part motion."*

### B.3 Reward from contact vs reward from keypoint distance

**DexMan's contact-prior attraction reward** — the full formula, because the object-frame construction is the whole trick:

```
Offline:  v^o_{j,t} = argmin_{v ∈ V_o} ‖p^hum_{j,t} − v‖₂           # nearest object mesh vertex to the NOISY human keypoint
          P(t) = {(j,o) : ‖p^hum_{j,t} − v^o_{j,t}‖₂ ≤ τ_j}         # keep only plausible contact pairs

Online:   R_contact(t) = (1/|O|) Σ_{(j,o)∈P(t)} w_c^j (1 + γ_c^j ⟨n^rob_{j,t}, d̂^o_{j,t}⟩)
                          · exp(−λ_c‖p^rob_{j,t} − v^o_{j,t}‖²₂) · (w_c1 + w_c2·1_lifted)
```

The correspondence lives in **object-local coordinates**, so the reward is invariant to global object-pose error — exactly the error monocular reconstruction makes. The `⟨n^rob, d̂⟩` term prevents back-of-hand contacts; `1_lifted` re-weights once the object leaves the surface. Total: `R = R_contact + R_obj + R_imit`, with a residual policy outputting `{Δx^h, Δω^h, Δθ^h}` through the same IK + PD as retargeting.

**PhysHOI's contact graph** ([2312.04393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04393) · [wyhuai/PhysHOI](https://github.com/wyhuai/PhysHOI)): complete graph `E ∈ {0,1}^{k(k−1)/2}`, reward `r^cg = exp(−Σ_j λ^cg[j]·e^cg[j])`, total reward **multiplicative** (`r = r^body · r^object · r^ig · r^cg` — "none of them may be small"). Its robustness trick: **aggregate nodes** (both hands as one node) to reduce sensitivity to per-part contact-annotation noise. The money quote for your design doc — without the contact-graph reward, kinematic rewards fall into a local optimum where the humanoid learns **"not to touch the object."**

**InterMimic's three-level contact reward** ([2502.20390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20390) · [Sirui-Xu/InterMimic](https://github.com/Sirui-Xu/InterMimic), CVPR'25, **SMPL-X and Unitree G1**, Isaac Gym + Isaac Lab): promotion / **neutral** / penalty markers, explicitly "to accommodate potential inaccuracies in reference contact distances." The neutral band is a deadband — where the reference is ambiguous, reward nothing rather than guess. Penalty threshold `σ_p = 0.1`.

**InterMimic's embodiment-aware weight** is the cleanest published "adaptive tracking tolerance":

```
w_d[i] = 0.5 · [ (1/‖d[i]‖²) / Σ_i(1/‖d[i]‖²) ] + 0.5 · [ (1/‖d̂[i]‖²) / Σ_i(1/‖d̂[i]‖²) ]
E_p = ⟨Δ_p, w_d⟩     E_θ = ⟨Δ_θ, 1 − w_d⟩     E_d = ⟨Δ_d, w_d⟩
```

Joints near the object get position-tracking priority; far joints get rotation priority. Spatially adaptive, costs nothing.

**HumanX** ([2602.02473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02473) · [wyhuai.github.io/human-x](https://wyhuai.github.io/human-x/), G1) uses a **body-object relative-motion reward** — `u^(k)_t = u^k_t − u^o_t` (body keypoint minus object position), plus relative rotation `R^(k) = R^obj (R^k)^{-1}`. Same principle as DexMan's object-local frame: the *relative* hand-object vector survives reconstruction noise that destroys the global trajectory.

### B.4 Robust losses inside the reward

- **RIL-Co** ([2010.10181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10181) · [voot-t/ril_co](https://github.com/voot-t/ril_co), AISTATS'21): symmetric loss condition `ℓ(z) + ℓ(−z) = c` is what makes the risk minimizer invariant to symmetric noise. They use the AP loss, not the unhinged loss (unbounded below → underfits).
- **Robust IL from Corrupted Demonstrations** ([2201.12594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12594)): **Median-of-Means** over minibatch groups — near-clean-data sample complexity under a constant fraction of *adversarially* corrupted demos. ~5 lines.
- **Adaptive t-Momentum** ([2108.00625](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00625), IROS'21): Student-t heavy-tailed modeling inside the optimizer, no need to know the outlier ratio.
- **Important caveat:** the GCE / symmetric-cross-entropy literature is *classification-specific* — `ℓ(z)+ℓ(−z)=c` doesn't transfer to continuous regression. For a tracking reward use redescending M-estimators (Huber, Tukey, Welsch). Note that `exp(−‖e‖²/σ²)` is already bounded and redescending, so you are partway there — which is another reason σ matters so much.

## C. Domain randomization / noise augmentation **over the reference**

### C.1 The honest state of the field

**PHC, H2O, OmniH2O, HumanPlus, ExBody2, GMT all claim robustness to noisy video-derived references and none of them inject noise into the reference during training** — verified against full texts. They get it from dynamics DR, fail-recovery, and *filtering the training data clean*. ExBody2 ([2412.13196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13196)) says the quiet part out loud: infeasible motions "introduce noise, degrading training effectiveness," so they remove them. That is a real, citable gap.

The papers that *do* train on corrupted references are all 2026, mostly code-free:

**RGMT — Robust and Generalized Humanoid Motion Tracking** — [2601.23080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.23080) · [project](https://zeonsunlightyu.github.io/RGMT.github.io/) · no code. G1, Isaac Gym, 5680 envs on one 4090, 3.5 h of data. Architecture: causal Transformer over K=10 proprio steps, max-pooled → dynamics embedding `h_t`, which becomes the **query for a cross-attention block over a ±L=10 reference-command window**. Attention weights conditioned on current dynamics, so the encoder down-weights command tokens inconsistent with what the robot is doing. Their noise table (scaled by a "noise level %"): `Δv_ref ~ U([−0.5,0.5]³)`, `Δω_ref ~ U([−0.52,0.52]³)` rad/s, `Δg_ref ~ U([−0.05,0.05]³)`, `Δq_ref ~ U([−0.1,0.1])` rad. **Baselines (GMT, Any2Track) collapse past 200%; RGMT holds to 1500%.** Ablation: replacing cross-attention with self-attention is the single biggest robustness loss — the cross-attention *is* the filter.

**OmniTrack** — [2602.23832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23832) · [omnitrack-humanoid.github.io](https://omnitrack-humanoid.github.io/) · no code. Two stages. Stage I: a privileged generalist (full global state, contacts, **deliberately loose ET threshold** because "overly strict termination would incorrectly treat many near-reference states as infeasible") rolls out the *raw noisy* reference. Explicit: **"We do not apply domain randomization in the physical motion generation stage. Instead, we inject noise into motion commands to encourage robust and stable rollouts across reference motions from diverse sources."** The rollout states — joints, global poses, velocities, contact states — become the new reference. Stage II trains the deployable proprio-only policy against those. Isaac Lab.

**CLOT** — [2602.15060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15060) · no code. **Randomized observation pre-shift**: shift the reference window the actor observes forward by `δ ∈ [0, T_max_pre]`, **compute the reward against the unshifted reference at time t**, disable at inference. Curriculum-scheduled. Temporal desync is a major monocular-reconstruction failure mode and nothing else addresses it directly. 20 lines.

**CLONE** — [2506.08931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08931) · [humanoid-clone.github.io](https://humanoid-clone.github.io/) · G1. The only physically-motivated reference-noise model: `dP_head = ṗ_head dt + (‖ṗ_head‖/c_vel + c_min) dW` — a Wiener process whose diffusion **scales with reference speed**, matching how odometry and monocular reconstruction actually degrade. Other body targets recomputed by FK from the randomized head, so kinematic consistency survives (iid per-joint noise destroys it).

**Heracles** — [2603.27756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27756) — the mirror case (noisy proprio, clean reference), worth citing for magnitude: removing it drops completion 90.6% → 78.6%, joint error +42%, **height error +91%**.

**CMP's OOD-Sensor recipe** — [2604.07457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07457) · [project](https://shepherd1226.github.io/CMP/) — at random events add a **persistent** bias for the rest of the trajectory: position drift `δ_p ~ N(0, 0.2²)` m, orientation `δ_r ~ N(0, 30²)°`. Persistent bias, not iid jitter, is what VIO/monocular failure looks like.

### C.2 The temporal-correlation finding (four independent confirmations)

Whatever noise you inject, **make it episode-level or temporally correlated, not per-step iid:**

- **MaskedMimic**: a sampled mask "has a chance of being repeated for multiple subsequent timesteps"; they explicitly report that re-sampling per step generalizes **worse**, because cross-frame visibility makes the request less ambiguous.
- **HoRD** ([2602.04412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04412), **trained in Isaac Lab**): sample DR params once per episode and hold. Under test-time DR, HoRD holds 84.4% success where MaskedMimic drops below 10%, OmniH2O 70.2%, ExBody2 69.4%.
- **BFMTrack**: pink noise (`S(f) ∝ 1/f`) for latent sequences — white noise "averages out over the trajectory" and kills the gradient signal.
- **Dexplore** ([2509.09671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09671)): episode-fixed latent ε.

### C.3 Noise-conditioned / quality-aware policies

No humanoid paper conditions on a scalar reference quality. Two papers hand you the parts:

**StableHand** — [2605.18553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18553) · no code. Per-frame, **per-component** quality `q_t = [q^L_W, q^L_F, q^R_W, q^R_F] ∈ [0,1]⁴` — wrist and fingers separated because their error scales decouple. GT quality: `e_W = ‖j̄₀ − j₀‖ + r₀·arccos[(tr(R̄ᵀR)−1)/2]` with MANO palm radius `r₀ = 85 mm`; `e_F` = MPJPE in the GT wrist frame. `q = exp(−e/σ)` with **σ calibrated so the worst 20% of frames land at q < 0.1**, i.e. `σ* = p₈₀(e)/ln 10`. At inference a Quality Network (temporal self-attention + RoPE, learnable `[geo]` token) predicts `log(1+ê)` **in mm, not q**, because mm is corpus-invariant while σ isn't.

**You have this supervision for free.** Tier-1 ↔ tier-2 pairs give you exact per-frame reference error labels. Nobody else in this literature has that.

**DexTrack's reference-quality triple** — computable from the reference alone, no GT:
- smoothness `t_s = (1/(N−1)) Σ_i (1/Δt)[w_s(s_{i+1}−s_i)/Δt − w_s(s_i−s_{i−1})/Δt]` (second difference → jitter)
- consistency `t_c = (1/(N−1)) Σ_i ‖(p^o_i − p^o_{i−1})/Δt − (p^h_i − p^h_{i−1})/Δt‖` (hand-vs-object velocity mismatch → broken grasp)
- penetration `t_p = (1/(N+1)) Σ_i PeneDepth(s_i, P^o)`

Cheap, windowable, and directly diagnostic of monocular failure. ~2 days to compute and append to obs.

**GigaBrain-WBC-0.5** — [2608.18234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18234) · no code · G1. Encodes a noisy masked reference window into `z`, with a training-only FSQ branch decoding back to the *clean* reference (cycle-consistency = denoising autoencoder on the reference). Deployment filter: pick the max-responsibility component `k*` of a 4-component GMM over the next command latent; if `M²_{k*}(z_raw) > R²_safe` (`R_safe = 3`), radially retract `z* = μ_{k*} + sqrt(R²_safe/M²_{k*})·(z_raw − μ_{k*})`. Keeps direction, clips magnitude. The filter alone, without the world-model head, is ~2 days and is your hard guard against tier-3 catastrophic frames.

**Context-aware DR lineage:** RMA [2107.04034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04034); UP-OSI (Yu et al., RSS 2017); Dynamics as Prompts [2410.20357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20357); Confidence-Conditioned Value Functions [2212.04607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04607) (ICLR'23 — one network representing the *whole family* of conservatism levels, choose at deployment).

### C.4 Observation-noise robustness theory

**SA-MDP / SA-PPO** — [2003.08938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08938) · [chenhongge/StateAdvDRL](https://github.com/chenhongge/StateAdvDRL), [huanzhang12/SA_PPO](https://github.com/huanzhang12/SA_PPO) · NeurIPS 2020. Formalizes exactly your setting: the adversary perturbs the *observation*, not the state. Results: an optimal policy under SA-MDP may not exist in general; the performance gap is bounded by the TV distance between `π(·|s)` and `π(·|ν(s))`. Practical output is a smoothness regularizer `max_{ŝ∈B(s)} D(π(·|s), π(·|ŝ))` on the PPO loss — ~40 lines with the cheap SGLD inner max. They report robust policies often improve *nominal* performance too.

Related: **ATLA** [2101.08452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.08452) · [huanzhang12/ATLA_robust_RL](https://github.com/huanzhang12/ATLA_robust_RL) (learned optimal adversary, ~2× cost); **PA-AD** [2106.05087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05087) (theoretically optimal attacker — use to *evaluate* your tiers); **RADIAL-RL** [2008.01976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.01976) · [tuomaso/radial_rl_v2](https://github.com/tuomaso/radial_rl_v2); **WocaR-RL** [2210.05927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05927) (worst-case-aware without running attacks); **Beyond Worst-case Attacks** [2402.12673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12673) (ICLR'24 — learns a *set* of non-dominated policies and adapts to actual attack strength at test time; conceptually the closest match to a three-tier evaluation).

**Nobody has applied SA-MDP to humanoid motion tracking.** Your three noise tiers are literally three perturbation-set radii. That's a paper.

### C.5 Test-time adaptation

**BFMTrack** — [2606.25056](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25056) · Disney Research Zurich · no code. Latent Sequence Optimization: optimize a *sequence* of latents at inference via sim rollouts + policy gradient. The essential trick is **temporally correlated pink noise** (`β=1`) for exploration. Budget: **24 iterations, N=128 parallel envs, Adam lr 6.25e-3, betas (0.8, 0.99), γ=0.97, σ=0.0125 — minutes per trajectory on one RTX 5090.** LSO as published needs a pretrained BFM; the transferable core is running the same optimization directly over your reference (or a spline/offset parameterization of it) with your own policy's rollouts as the objective.

**BRIC** — [2511.20431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20431) · [bric2026.github.io](https://bric2026.github.io/). Test-time adaptation to noisy diffusion motion plans, with two safeguards worth stealing: (a) **EMA anti-forgetting** — maintain `θ' ← αθ' + (1−α)θ` and add `L_CF = (V(s;θ_V) − V(s;θ'_V))² + ‖μ(s;θ_π) − μ(s;θ'_π)‖² + (D − D')²`; (b) signal-space guidance `x₀ ← x₀ − η∇_{x₀}L_guide(Ψ(x₀))`, no backprop through the diffusion model. Without (a), adapting to tier-3 will wreck your tier-1 score.

**Bi-Level Motion Imitation** — [2410.01968](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01968) · CoRL 2024 · [site](https://sites.google.com/view/bmi-corl2024). Alternates optimizing the policy *and* the target mocap. A self-consistent autoencoder learns sparse structured representations that capture general patterns "rather than nuances and noise"; latent dynamics then generate modified references. The most principled published "fix the reference with the policy in the loop."

**RePHO** (CVPR 2026) — [2606.05359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05359) · [site](https://dingbang777.github.io/RePHO/). Most on-point 2026 physics-cleanup work: *"Because kinematic estimates are typically noisy, naive RL training can fail. Therefore we propose an adaptive sampling strategy with a dual self-updating mechanism that can identify the frames with the most informative and reliable kinematic reconstruction."* Confidence-gated trust in the reference, per frame.

### C.6 Perception-noise modeling for sim

Thin. Nobody has published a calibrated 6D-pose-estimator error model for policy training. Best available: **ComPose** [2605.23523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23523) (read this to understand the *shape* of tier-3 error — occlusion-driven, temporally bursty, hand-correlated, **not Gaussian**); **Benchmarking Effects of Object Pose Estimation on Grasping Success** [2602.17101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17101) (empirical error→success curves); **BOP Challenge 2024** [2504.02812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02812) (per-method error distributions you can fit); **HOT3D** [2411.19167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19167) (GT for calibrating synthetic corruption). CMP's persistent-bias recipe and CLONE's velocity-scaled SDE are the two best *usable* models.

## D. Learning from imperfect / suboptimal demonstrations

**The structural mismatch, stated once:** most of this literature exists to *infer a reward you don't have*. You have a dense reward. So T-REX/D-REX/SSRR/Bayesian-REX, the GAIL-confidence family, and the DICE family are mostly dead weight — what transfers is the **weighting / confidence / filtering machinery**, which in each paper is a small separable component. A second mismatch: your "demonstration" is a state-only, per-timestep *target*, and the noise is in the target, not in the actions.

### D.3 Robust IL under noise

**RIL-Co** ([2010.10181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10181) · [voot-t/ril_co](https://github.com/voot-t/ril_co), AISTATS'21): symmetric loss + co-pseudo-labeling (two classifiers on disjoint splits, each trained on peer pseudo-labels, suppressing self-confirmation bias); `λ=0.5` derived, not tuned. **TREND** ([2505.06079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06079), ICRA'25) extends to three reward models with the **small-loss criterion** — "samples your model already fits well are probably clean" — and holds ~90% success at **40% preference noise**. **BCND** (ICLR'21, [OpenReview zrT3HcsWSAt](https://openreview.net/forum?id=zrT3HcsWSAt), no arXiv, no code): weight each demo by *your own current policy's* likelihood — label-free, but the follow-up literature reports it's a positive-feedback loop and unstable without an ensemble.

### D.5 2024–2026 noisy-demo work

- **Restoring Noisy Demonstration for IL with Diffusion Models** — [2510.14467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14467) (IEEE TNNLS). Filter-then-**restore**: train conditional diffusion on the clean subset to reconstruct the noisy ones. Reference trajectories are exactly the smooth structured signal diffusion denoises well.
- **DP-IL** — [2310.07143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07143) · [yunke-wang/dp-il](https://github.com/yunke-wang/dp-il) · ICML'24. **Add more noise, then denoise**: forward-diffuse to `t*` (smooths existing noise into tractable Gaussian noise), then reverse. Theorem 2 bounds the L2 distance to the optimal demo. Pairs naturally with C.1 — you already have a forward noise model.
- **SMILE** — [2310.15815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15815). Diffusion timesteps as an expertise-gap metric; when `t(τ)=0` the demo is no better than the agent and is auto-filtered.
- **Counterfactual BC** — [2505.10760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10760). Treat the reference as a *region* of acceptable behavior, not a point target. Widen the tolerance tube instead of chasing a noisy point.
- **GR-RL** — [2512.01801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01801). Production-scale confirmation of the CRR recommendation: filters demos using offline RL with sparse rewards, **treating the learned Q-value as a progress function**. 83.3% on autonomous shoe-lacing.
- **Skill Disentanglement for IL from Suboptimal Demos** — [2306.07919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07919); **SSDF** — [2401.08957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08957) (ICRA'25, recycles the good *parts* of failed trajectories); **EIL** — [2210.01379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01379); **NAC** — [1802.05313](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05313) (no separate imitation loss ⇒ **not forced to mimic every example** — the property you want).

**No dedicated 2024–2026 survey on suboptimal demonstrations exists.** Closest: [2106.12177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.12177) (Zheng et al., IEEE TNNLS — the only one that explicitly commits to suboptimal-demo policy learning), [2511.03565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03565) (most current general IL survey), [2509.19379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19379) (learning-from-observation).

### D.6 Is DAgger even applicable here?

**No, not directly, and the literature confirms it** — there is essentially no work running DAgger with a raw retargeted kinematic trajectory as the oracle. Three preconditions fail:

1. **The reference is not a policy.** DAgger needs `π*: S → A` evaluable at *arbitrary learner-visited states*. A retargeted trajectory `q̂_{1:T}` is a time-indexed open-loop signal defined only at `t`. Once the humanoid drifts off — the only regime where DAgger earns its keep — `q̂_t` is the expert's action at a state the expert is not in.
2. **Wrong action space.** `q̂` is configuration-space; your action is torque or PD target. Any label you manufacture is *your controller's* output, not the expert's.
3. **The expert is infeasible.** DAgger's O(Tε) bound needs small expert-class error. A retargeted human motion is generally dynamically infeasible for G1.

What the field does instead — four patterns:

- **(A) Promote the reference into a state-conditioned kinematic policy.** This *is* KinPoly/EmbodiedPose and it *is* DAgger, though neither cites it. KinPoly's dynamics-regulated training (Alg. 2) feeds the kinematic policy the **simulated** pose `q_t` rather than the auto-regressive `q̃_t`, supervised by the original mocap GT: on-policy states, off-policy expert labels. The authors call it "a powerful data augmentation step."
- **(B) Reference-as-reward** (DeepMimic lineage), with RSI + ET substituting for on-policy correction.
- **(C) Reference-as-nominal-controller + learned residual** (§A.4).
- **(D) DAgger between two *learned* policies** — a privileged RL teacher is queryable, so the precondition holds. **GBC** ([2508.09960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09960) · [sjtu-mvasl-robotics/rsl_rl](https://github.com/sjtu-mvasl-robotics/rsl_rl)) is instructive: they train `π_D` on retargeted data in a *simplified fixed-base* env first, precisely because the reference isn't queryable, then use `π_D` as the oracle with `L_CLIP + w_im·L_KL`. Also VIRAL [2511.15200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15200), FADA [2606.28476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28476), TrajBooster [2509.11839](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11839).

## E. Practical tricks for noisy retargeted hand-object data

### E.1 Feasibility filtering — how real systems drop untrackable motions

**H2O's "sim-to-data"** ([2403.04436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04436) · [LeCAR-Lab/human2humanoid](https://github.com/LeCAR-Lab/human2humanoid)) is the canonical recipe, quoted: *"we first retarget the human motions to the humanoid via IK, and train a humanoid controller with access to privileged state information to imitate the unfiltered motions in simulation. Afterwards, we remove the motion sequences that the privileged imitator fails to track."* **No DR** in the filter policy — it's a feasibility oracle, not a deployable. Result: ~8.5k of 10k AMASS survive for H1. Ships as `amass_phc_filtered.pkl`, plus `grad_fit_h1_shape.py` for the shape-fitting step that prevents in-toed artifacts.

Others: **GMT** ([2506.14770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14770) · [zixuan417/humanoid-general-motion-tracking](https://github.com/zixuan417/humanoid-general-motion-tracking)) — rule-based (root roll/pitch thresholds, abnormal root height) then policy-based; 8925 clips / 33.12 h survive. Sampler worth copying verbatim: `p_i = (min(E_max_key_body_error / 0.15, 1))^5` — the `^5` aggressively concentrates on near-failures. **ExBody2** ([2412.13196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13196)) — teacher produces intermediate data that conforms to robot kinematics and auto-filters infeasible motions. **TWIST** ([2505.02833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02833) · [YanjieZe/TWIST](https://github.com/YanjieZe/TWIST), Isaac Gym + **rsl_rl, G1 with hands**) — `L = L_RL + λ·D_KL(π_stu ‖ π_tea)` with λ annealed; teacher gets **2 s of future reference** as privilege. **TEXEDO** ([2606.22998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22998)) — the closest thing to an explicit **trackability score**: a dynamic feasibility verifier distilled from tracking rollouts, used as a hard constraint at test time. **SPIDER's contact filter** is the best one for video-derived hand data: *if a contact duration is shorter than `t_c,min` or the contact point drifts more than `d_c,max`, the contact is unstable and its virtual constraint is disabled* — an automatic hallucinated-contact detector.

### E.3 RSI and early termination under noise

**RSI's failure mode under noise, named by InterMimic:** *"initializing with the imperfect reference can introduce critical artifacts, such as contact floating or incorrect hand motion, leading to unrecoverable failures, e.g. object falling. These issues render many initializations ineffective... The problem is exacerbated by the use of prioritized sampling, which favors high-failure-rate initializations."*

The fix appears independently in four papers under four names — all the same idea:

| Name | Paper | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **PSI** (Physical State Initialization) | InterMimic | FIFO buffer mixing reference states with *simulation* states whose discounted return exceeded a threshold |
| **Adaptive mid-trajectory reset library** | ConTrack | Per-frame simulator states from **policy-reachable** rollouts; EMA continuation length `ℓ̄_k` drives difficulty-aware sampling. Resets migrate backward as the reachable suffix grows |
| **Progressive state pool** | SUGAR | Initialize from physically-validated states instead of unreliable references |
| **ε-NSI** | SkillMimic-V2 | Sample `s_new` uniformly from an ε-neighborhood of `ŝ_i`, find `ŝ_j = argmax_s S(s_new, s)`, build `{s_new, s_∅×N, ŝ_j, …, ŝ_T}` with N masked bridge frames |

ConTrack quantifies it: adaptive library 0.899 progress / 0.026 m obj err, vs reset-from-start 0.700 / 0.039, vs uniform 0.727 / 0.033.

**Early termination schedules actually used:**

| Work | Mechanism | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| ASAP | termination-distance curriculum | **1.5 m → 0.3 m** over training |
| ManipTrans | per-quantity ET curriculum | finger 6 cm → 4 cm; object 90°/6 cm → 30°/2 cm |
| GMT | joint decay of `E_i` and completion level `c_i` | `E_min = 0.25`, `c_min = 1.0` |
| PHC | **RET** | 0.5 m mean joint distance, **ankle+toe excluded** |
| MaskedMimic | terrain-conditioned | 0.25 m flat, 0.5 m irregular |
| OmniTrack Stage I | deliberately loose | "relatively large ET threshold... overly strict termination would incorrectly treat many near-reference states as infeasible" |
| InterMimic | **IET** | ET + (i) object points >0.5 m avg from ref, (ii) joint-to-object-surface >0.5 m from ref, (iii) **any required contact lost >10 consecutive frames** |
| ResMimic | object-mesh ET | `‖P_t − P̂_t‖₂ > σ_o`, or required contact lost >10 frames |
| HumanX | **probabilistic** Interaction Termination | when the reference frame involves contact and object-to-key-body relative error exceeds threshold, terminate *with probability* — "crucial for achieving stable real-world deployment" |
| BeyondMimic | **Z-only** position thresholds | anchor 0.25 m (z-only), ori 0.8, EE bodies 0.25 m (z-only) — horizontal drift doesn't kill the episode |

**Nobody has published ET-threshold-vs-reference-noise-magnitude.** But the pattern is unambiguous: *nobody uses a fixed tight threshold with imperfect references.* Either start loose and tighten, or keep termination loose and put the tightening in the reward temperature (KungfuBot), or exclude the joints your retargeter gets wrong. For you that means **exclude fingers from termination entirely** and terminate on the object.

### E.4 The "virtual object" trick — probably the highest-value single item

**DexMachina** ([2505.24853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24853) · [MandiZhao/dexmachina](https://github.com/MandiZhao/dexmachina)): every object gets six virtual 1-DoF base joints plus an articulation joint, all PD-actuated toward the demonstration target. Early in training the object *is moved for you*; the policy only has to learn plausible contact. Gains decay on a **reward-gated** trigger: when `μ_z > σ_z` for all `z ∈ {task, imi, bc, con}`, `k_p ← k_p·φ_p`, halting at `k_p ≤ 0.01`. Task reward is multiplicative, `r_task = r_pos·r_rot·r_angle`. The authors state the objective plainly: auxiliary rewards are "soft guidance" so that "the policy [can] deviate from the reference hand motions... to prioritize optimizing for high task rewards."

**ResMimic** does the same on G1 and its ablation names your failure mode exactly:

```
F_t = k_p(p̂^o_t − p^o_t) − k_d v^o_t ,   T_t = k_p(θ̂^o_t ⊖ θ^o_t) − k_d ω^o_t     # gains decayed
```

> *"In this task, the reference motions contain imperfections, including penetrations between the humanoid's hand and the object. The policy initially focuses only on motion tracking to reach the object, causing the object to be knocked over, which yields low object rewards and frequent early terminations. This quickly drives the policy into a local minimum where **the robot retreats rather than engaging with the object**. In contrast, with the virtual force curriculum, the object remains stabilized during early learning, enabling the policy to overcome motion-data imperfections."*

**SPIDER** does the annealed-constraint version inside a sampling optimizer instead of RL (`η_i → ∞ as i → N`).

### E.5 Reference augmentation that preserves contact

- **REGRIND's `ΔT(t)` warp** — perturb initial object pose (±5 cm, ±30° yaw) → rigid `ΔT₀ ∈ SE(3)`, then apply a time-varying warp interpolating from `ΔT₀` at t=0 to identity at the goal, to **both** the object pose and the hand root at every step. Relative spatial/contact structure preserved exactly, unlimited references, zero optimization. **2–3 days.**
- **InterReal** ([2603.07516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07516), **Unitree G1**) — its empirical finding matches your setup: *"sensor disturbances in human and object trajectories, particularly **relative position perturbations between them**, can easily drive the learned policy out of distribution or even cause collapse."* Fix: offset the object by `Δp_xy` on a grid and **re-solve IK for both arms** in the pelvis frame so contact stays consistent. Plus an automatic reward learner — a meta-policy allocating reward weights from critical tracking-error metrics.
- **HumanX's XGen** — scale object geometry, enrich object trajectories in contact and non-contact phases; **disturbed initialization** perturbs root rotation/displacement, joint angles *and object pose* each episode; the student **receives no phase or reference data as observations** to prevent trajectory overfitting.
- **ContactMimic** ([2607.08742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08742) · [page](https://lixinyao11.github.io/contactmimic-page/)) — tracks explicit **part-level binary contact commands** alongside keypoints, with trajectory augmentation "aimed at breaking the correlations between keypoint trajectories and contact labels." Opens with your exact observation: *"keypoint tracking alone is insufficient... the robot can reach the correct pose without making meaningful physical contact."*
- **DeVI** ([2604.20841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20841) · [snuvclab.github.io/devi](https://snuvclab.github.io/devi/)) — **hybrid tracking reward integrating 3D human tracking with robust 2D object tracking**. When 3D lifting is unreliable, fall back to an image-space objective for the object. A genuinely novel hedge against depth/scale error.
- **TopoRetarget** ([2606.16272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16272)) — sparse interaction graph over hand+object keypoints, distance-weighted Laplacian deformation with directional consistency + kinematic constraints + penetration handling. **+40.6 pp** on Pen-Spin training success over baselines; zero-shot to WUJI Hand. **OmniRetarget** ([2509.26633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26633) · [amazon-far/holosoma](https://github.com/amazon-far/holosoma) · [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/omniretarget/OmniRetarget_Dataset)) builds the interaction mesh **in the object's local frame** so hand-object relations are invariant to object transforms; downstream policy then needs only 5 reward terms.

## What I'd actually implement in 6 weeks — ranked, as specific algorithmic changes

**Fork REGRIND for the env/reward structure and BeyondMimic for the Isaac Lab tracking scaffold. Retarget with GMR (`unitree_g1_with_hands`). Don't write a retargeter.**

### Tier 0 — do in week 1, each is a day or less, each independently ablatable

**1. Make the object reward an ADD-style point-cloud reward.** Replace `exp(−λ_p‖p−p̂‖) + w·exp(−λ_θ‖θ⊖θ̂‖)` with ResMimic's `r^o = exp(−λ_o Σ_{i=1}^N ‖P[i]_t − P̂[i]_t‖₂)` over N mesh-surface points. You are scored on a point-set distance; reward one. Kills the pos/rot weight-tuning problem and smooths the landscape. Use the same quantity for termination (`‖P_t − P̂_t‖ > σ_o`).

**2. Demote the hand reference.** Set `w_obj,pos = 1.0`, `w_obj,rot = 0.3`, `w_wrist ≈ 0.1`, and **drop finger-joint tracking from the reward entirely** (WristMimic) — fingers learn from object tracking + contact. Within a contact window `[t_c − t_b, t_c + t_a]`, zero the arm-joint reward weight, keep wrist at 1, reduce the rest. Exclude fingers from termination (PHC's RET, applied to your morphology). This is the single biggest expected gain on tiers 2–3 and it is ~2 hours of config.

**3. Asymmetric reference channel.** Actor observes the corrupted reference; critic observes the clean one; **reward is computed against the clean tier-1 target**. BeyondMimic's `PolicyCfg`/`PrivilegedCfg` split already does the observation half — you just raise the `Unoise` magnitude and point the reward at the GT. This is SoftMimic's `q_ref` observed / `q_aug` rewarded pattern, and you have the paired data for free. Nobody else in this literature does.

**4. Make all injected noise episode-level / temporally correlated.** Sample the corruption parameters once per episode and hold. Four independent papers (MaskedMimic, HoRD, BFMTrack, Dexplore) report per-step iid is worse.

### Tier 1 — weeks 1–2, the things that decide whether training converges at all

**5. Decaying virtual-object PD controller.** `F_t = k_p(p̂^o − p^o) − k_d v^o`, `T_t = k_p(θ̂^o ⊖ θ^o) − k_d ω^o`, with **reward-gated** decay (DexMachina: when all reward families clear their thresholds, `k_p ← k_p·φ_p`, stop at 0.01) rather than a fixed schedule. This is the difference between a policy that manipulates the object and one that learns to retreat from it — ResMimic's ablation demonstrates exactly that failure under references containing hand-object penetrations. ~3 days. **If you fall behind, this is the item to keep.**

**6. Adaptive reward tolerance: `σ ← min(σ, EMA(tracking_error))`.** KungfuBot's derivation says the optimal σ is the mean achievable error; the closed loop finds it. σ non-increasing, initialized large. Their ablation shows no fixed σ works across motions of differing quality — which is precisely your three-tier problem. **~5 lines**, and it removes a hyperparameter you cannot tune three times.

**7. H2O feasibility filter.** One privileged, no-DR policy trained on everything; one deterministic eval pass; keep the survivors as your training index. Expect to drop 15–25%. KungfuBot's numbers say rejects cap at 54% episode-length ratio, so this is cheap information. One training run.

**8. Object-frame contact-prior reward (DexMan).** Extract `(hand keypoint j → nearest object mesh vertex v)` correspondences from the *noisy* reference, filter by `τ_j`, reward the robot for realizing that correspondence **in object-local coordinates**, with the `⟨n^rob, d̂⟩` normal-alignment term and the `1_lifted` gate. Object-local ⇒ invariant to the global pose error monocular reconstruction makes. Coarsen the contact graph (PhysHOI: `{L palm, L fingers, R palm, R fingers, object}`, not per-fingertip) because per-fingertip labels from video are not trustworthy. ~1 week.

### Tier 2 — weeks 2–4

**9. Policy-reachable reset library, not RSI on the raw reference.** Maintain a per-frame buffer of simulator states from successful rollouts (ConTrack's EMA continuation length `ℓ̄_k` for difficulty-aware sampling; InterMimic's return-thresholded FIFO). Resetting *to the reference* is physically inconsistent when contacts are wrong. ConTrack: 0.899 vs 0.700 progress, 0.026 vs 0.039 m object error. ~100 lines.

**10. Object-tracking-as-constraint with a dual variable (ConTrack).** `λ ← λ + η(α − Ĵ_g/J_g*)`, `w_task ← σ(λ)`, `A_mix ← w_task·A_g + (1−w_task)·A_s + A_p` — mixing **advantages**, not rewards, which in rsl_rl means separate advantage streams or a multi-head critic. Their sweep over α traces a Pareto frontier that dominates every fixed mixing. This is the principled answer to "how much should I trust the hand reference," and it *adapts per clip* — so one policy handles all three tiers. Two days of plumbing, a few days of tuning α.

**11. Contact-preserving reference augmentation.** REGRIND's `ΔT(t)` warp first (2–3 days, zero optimization): perturb the initial object pose ±5 cm / ±30° yaw, apply a time-varying SE(3) warp decaying to identity at the goal, to **both** object and hand root. Then InterReal's IK-re-solved object offsets if time permits. Both broaden the reference distribution without breaking contact — unlike raw Gaussian noise on joint targets.

**12. Structured, not isotropic, reference corruption (S3RR's point).** Build your tier-2 generator from failure modes, not from `N(0,σ)`: persistent root drift (CMP: `δ_p ~ N(0,0.2²)` m, `δ_r ~ N(0,30²)°`, applied from a random event onward), velocity-scaled diffusion on the wrist (CLONE's SDE, with the rest recomputed by FK so kinematic consistency survives), temporal desync (CLOT's pre-shift), and occlusion-bursty object-pose dropout (ComPose's characterization). Then also: CLOT's **observation pre-shift with unshifted reward** is 20 lines and nothing else addresses timing error.

**13. Segment-level adaptive sampling.** BeyondMimic's `_adaptive_sampling` (already in the repo) or GMT's `p_i = (min(E/0.15, 1))^5`. Concentrates compute on the frames where the reference is worst.

### Tier 3 — weeks 4–6, pick one or two

**14. Reference quality conditioning.** Cheap version (2 days): compute DexTrack's `(t_s, t_c, t_p)` triple in a sliding window and append to the observation — no GT needed. Expensive version (~1 week): a StableHand-style Quality Network regressing per-frame reference error **in mm**, trained on your tier-1↔tier-2 pairs, with σ calibrated so the worst 20% land at `q < 0.1`. Feed `q̂` to the policy. Keep wrist and finger quality as **separate channels** — their error scales decouple. **You have paired supervision that nobody in this literature has; this is the most defensible novelty in the whole plan.**

**15. Advantage-weighted reference term (CRR).** Weight the imitation/tracking term by `exp(Â(s,a)/β)` or `1[Â > 0]`. You already train a critic. Automatically suppresses reference frames the policy can already beat — the noisy ones. GR-RL validates this at production scale as a "Q-as-progress-function" filter. ~1 day. Highest value-per-line in Tier 3.

**16. Teacher-student with a clean-reference teacher.** Teacher: full object state + privileged contacts + TWIST's 2 s of future reference. Student: noisy reference only, 25-step proprio history (OmniH2O's ablated optimum), no global linear velocity. Loss `‖a^teacher − a^student‖²`, then TWIST's `L_RL + λ·D_KL(π_stu ‖ π_tea)` with λ annealed. If you want the most principled version, ULTRA's privileged-posterior / masked-prior KL — but budget a full week and it collapses easily.

**17. Robust loss hygiene.** Median-of-Means over minibatch groups of the tracking loss (~5 lines, adversarial-corruption robustness from [2201.12594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12594)). Note `exp(−‖e‖²/σ²)` is already bounded and redescending, so item 6 is doing most of this work — but MoM is orthogonal and free.

**18. Test-time reference refinement.** Pink-noise (`β=1`) sequence optimization over a spline/offset parameterization of the reference, using your policy's own rollouts: 24 iterations × 128 envs, Adam 6.25e-3, minutes per trajectory (BFMTrack's budget). Guard with BRIC's EMA-consistency loss or tier-3 adaptation will cost you tier-1 score.

### The escape hatch, if tier-3 references are so broken nothing converges

Run **OmniTrack's Stage I first, not last**: a privileged generalist (full state, contacts, deliberately *loose* ET, noise injected on the motion command, **no DR**) rolls out the raw reference; the rollout states become your new reference; train the deployable policy on those. Their Table A.8 gives +3 to +4.4 pp success and 2.7–6.4 mm MPJPE, measured **against the raw reference**, with the gap widening as the dataset grows. Same family as H2O's sim-to-data, InterMimic's reference distillation, and SUGAR's progressive state pool. Budget 2–3 weeks.

### Cut list

PMCP's multi-primitive network surgery; PULSE/BFM latent spaces; SkillMimic-V2's STG graph construction; ASAP's delta-action model (needs real-robot rollouts); full CMP; ATLA-style learned adversaries (2× cost over SA-PPO for marginal gain at your scale); MoE policies unless a single MLP visibly saturates; **RFC in any form**.

### One framing note for the writeup

The gap is real and citable. PHC, H2O, OmniH2O, HumanPlus, ExBody2, and GMT all claim robustness to noisy video-derived references while training exclusively on *filtered clean* data. The 2026 papers that genuinely train on corrupted references — RGMT, CLOT, OmniTrack — have no released code, and none of them handle hand-object interaction. Nobody has applied SA-MDP to humanoid motion tracking, and nobody has published an ET-threshold-vs-reference-noise study. A graded-noise benchmark scored on *object* tracking accuracy, on a G1 + Dex3, is unoccupied.
