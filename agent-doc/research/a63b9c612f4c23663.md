### A4. YAHMP's tuning findings — the single most usable page in Part A

From https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19903 (Unitree G1, zero-shot hardware):
- **Include reference joint velocities** in the motion command. Removing them costs 7–14% base error, ~9% key-body error, 15% joint-velocity error; the history encoder can't recover it.
- **History H=10.** H=0 costs +38–50% base error; H=20 gains 1–4% and worsens some metrics.
- **Residual actions** around reference positions: only 5–6% gain. Marginal.
- **Mechanics-based PD gains**: `kp,j = I_j·ω₀²`, `kd,j = 2ζI_j·ω₀` with ω₀ = 2π·10 rad/s, ζ = 2. A stiffer fixed scale raises max torques 13% overall / 41% on the upper body for no tracking gain.
- **Hand-force randomization** (0.5–2.0 s, capped 20 N): near-free on free-space tracking, and policies then sustain 5–6 kg vs failing at 4 kg; elbow droop 15.5° → 6.6°.
- **Teacher-student buys only 6–8%** over plain PPO with deployable observations. Skip it.
- DR ranges: foot-ground friction [0.3, 1.2], torso COM ±0.025–0.05 m, base pushes every 1–3 s.

### BeyondMimic `whole_body_tracking` — study this even if you don't fork it
https://github.com/HybridRobotics/whole_body_tracking — MIT, Isaac Lab 2.1.0, rsl_rl. Deploy: [HybridRobotics/motion_tracking_controller](https://github.com/HybridRobotics/motion_tracking_controller) (ROS 2).

### C2. Object-centric reward design — the canonical term set

**1. Object pose tracking.** Two camps.
- *Product of exponentials* — ObjDex → DexMachina → PhysHOI / InterMimic / DeVI:
  `r_task = exp(−β_p·d_pos)·exp(−β_r·d_rot)·exp(−β_a·d_ang)`. DexMachina's stated reason: a product can't be gamed by maxing one component. Their shipped values: `--task_rew_betas 10 1 5`, `-imi 0.3 -bc 0.3 -con 3`.
- *Weighted sum* — Weave, GRAIL, CHORD, TopoRetarget, ResMimic, HDMI. Easier per-term σ tuning.
- *Geometric replacements that match an ADD-style score better*: **ResMimic's point-cloud reward** `exp(−λ·Σ_i‖P_i − P̂_i‖₂)` over N mesh surface points (*"smoother reward landscape… naturally accounts for both translation and rotation, eliminating the need for task-specific weight tuning"*); TopoRetarget's 6 object axis-points at weight **8× everything else**; REGRIND's object keypoints; DeVI's 2D image-space projection.
- **Frame matters enormously.** InterMimic computes object pose in the humanoid heading frame; HDMI, TopoRetarget, Weave and GRAIL all use root-frame object observations. GRAIL measured the cost of absolute-frame observations: **−23.5 pp SR** (57.9% vs 81.4%).

**2. Contact.** The highest-leverage single term.
- Position-matched (DexMachina, mask-gated L2, `D = d_max` on mask disagreement, `contact_beta 10–30`).
- Force-magnitude-matched (Weave: `Σ_h m_h(1 − |y_h − c̃_h|)/(Σm+ε)`, `c̃ = min(‖f‖/5N, 1)`, ternary reference labels {−1,0,+1}).
- Force-thresholded (ManipTrans `exp(−λ_c/Σ_f C_f·1[d<ξ_c])`; HDMI multiplies by `max(exp((‖F‖−F_thres)/σ), 1)`).
- Contact-time saturating (GRAIL `min(N_contact/N_min, 1)` at weight **40.0**).
- Object-frame vertex attraction with **normal alignment** (DexMan `(1 + γ⟨n_rob, d̂⟩)·exp(−λ‖p_rob − v_obj‖²)` — the `⟨n,d̂⟩` factor kills back-of-hand contacts).
- Contact graph + contact-*energy* penalty `exp(−w‖F‖²)` to stop slamming (PhysHOI / InterMimic).
- **Contact-wrench space (CHORD)** — compares support functions of human vs robot contact-wrench matrices over `b` sampled 6-D directions with tolerance β, plus explicit unintended/missed-contact penalties. Motivation: a contact can be spatially right but have a normal nearly perpendicular to the demo's, producing no useful wrench.
- **Ablation magnitudes:** DexMan TACO SR 27.4% → **7.2%** without contact reward. CHORD box-grab 0.702 (wrench) vs 0.334 (position-only) vs 0.384 (none). ManipTrans: contact in reward improves SR, as observation accelerates convergence, as termination is critical for convergence at all.

**3. Hand opposition / force closure.** InterPrior origin: `u_T = (p_T−q_T)/‖·‖`, `e_h = 1 − (1/|S|)Σ_j (1 − u_Tᵀu_j)/2`, `r_h = exp(−w_h·e_h)`, with `w_h` growing as hand-object distance shrinks. Reference-free — it uses the *live* simulated object pose, so it still guides fingers when the reference is wrong. Weave adopts it at weight 2.0; GRAIL's `[−cos(d_thumb, d_index)]⁺` is the 2-finger version that **degenerates gracefully to Dex3's thumb + {index, middle}**. CHORD's reduced objective `r_fc = (1/B)Σ 1[σ_{r,b} > ε]` is equivalent to force closure when maximized, used when contact estimates are noisy.

**4. BC to retargeted joints.** DexMachina `r_bc = (1/J)Σ exp(−β_bc‖q̂−q‖₂)` with **β_bc = 500** (very sharp) at λ=0.3. Always down-weighted, by design: *"the policy can deviate from the reference hand motions… in order to prioritize optimizing for high task rewards."*

**5. Virtual Object Controller (VOC) / decaying assistive forces.**

| System | Mechanism | Annealing |
|---|---|---|
| **DexMachina** (origin) | 6 virtual 1-DoF base joints + 1 articulation joint, PD-actuated to the demo | **Reward-gated exponential**: decay `kp ← kp·φp` only when *all four* normalized rewards clear `σ = {task 0.5, con 0.05, imi 0.05, bc 0.05}` over a 100-episode deque; `kp ≤ 0.01 → 0`; hard zero at 30,000 epochs; ×0.98 dialback if episode length collapses. Init `kp=1000, kv=10, force_range=50` (example script 80/5). |
| **ResMimic** | Same, cites DexMachina | "gradually decayed." Ablation: without VOC the policy *"retreats rather than engages"* — a hard local minimum. |
| **CHORD / V2D** | DexMachina VOC | Reset to arbitrary reference frames, **keep VOC fully active for a stabilization window**, then anneal on a curriculum. Stage 2 decays to zero; stage 3 turns it off. |
| **ManipTrans** | Physics relaxation instead of forces | gravity `9.81 → 0.005`, friction `5.0 → 1.0`, plus ET thresholds `ε_finger 6→4 cm`, `ε_object 90°→30°`, `6→2 cm`. *"Without initial relaxation… the network may fail to converge entirely."* |
| **REGRIND** | gravity 0 → g | — |
| **FALCON** | Inverse: **escalating** torque-limit-aware 3D force curriculum α_g: 0→1 | progressive |
| **Dexplore** | No forces — anneals ET thresholds `κ = κ_init·N_fail/N_total` | online, failure-rate-adaptive |

**Rule of thumb:** decaying assistive object forces and relaxed physics are interchangeable in intent, but **reward-gated schedules are strictly safer than time-based** — a time-based decay that outpaces learning collapses into the "retreat" minimum.

**Weave's complete config** (`configs/track/train.yaml`, the best public worked example, `w·exp(−e/σ²)` form):
```yaml
motion_anchor_pos: {w: 1.0, std: 0.3}   motion_anchor_ori: {w: 1.0, std: 0.4}
motion_body_pos:   {w: 1.0, std: 0.3}   motion_body_ori:   {w: 1.0, std: 0.4}
object_pos:        {w: 2.0, std: 0.2}   object_ori:        {w: 2.0, std: 0.3}
hand_opposition:   {w: 2.0, thumb_body_name: thumb_distal,
                    finger_body_names: [index_intermediate, middle_intermediate,
                                        ring_intermediate, pinky_intermediate]}
contact:           {w: 2.0, saturate_force: 5.0}
feet_air_time: 0.5   feet_slide: -1.0   action_rate_l2: -0.1   joint_limit: -10.0
terminations: anchor_pos 0.25 | anchor_ori 0.8 | object_pos 0.30 | object_ori 0.3
              ee_body_pos 0.25 (ankles + wrists) | bad_contact max_lost_frames 10
```
Object terms are **2× body terms**. For Dex3 the `finger_body_names` list collapses to index + middle (drop ring/pinky).

**HDMI's table** for comparison: Body Local Pose 2.0, Root Global Pose 1.0, Body Global Velocity 1.0, Joint Tracking 1.0, **Object Pose 2.0, Contact 5.0**, Action Rate L2 0.1, Joint Limits L1 10.0, Feet Impact 1.0, Feet Slip 0.5, Feet Air Time 5.0. Terminations 0.5 m / 1.2 rad on root, body-local and object, plus contact loss.

**GRAIL's:** motion anchor pos/ori 0.5 / 2.5, relative body pos/ori 1.0 / 5.0, velocities 1.0, **object pos+ori 20.0**, contact count 40.0, plus thumb-index opposition and fingertip-to-contact-centroid terms, all gated by a per-frame contact indicator.

### C3. How whole-body balance interferes with manipulation tracking

**This is the best-evidenced claim in the whole literature. Five groups measured it independently.**

**GRAIL's ablation is the cleanest single data point** (124 motions, 43 objects, SR = mean object position error < 20 cm):

| Variant | SR↑ | ObjPos↓ | MPJPE-L↓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| HDMI | 48.5% | 0.283 | 122.3 |
| ResMimic | 49.2% | 0.393 | 80.9 |
| w/o SONIC (from scratch) | 45.0% | 0.395 | 243.5 |
| **w/o adaptor (vanilla SONIC)** | **39.7%** | 0.303 | **37.1 ← best body tracking** |
| w/o relative object obs | 57.9% | 0.257 | 43.0 |
| **GRAIL full** | **81.4%** | **0.135** | 41.8 |

The variant with the *best* body tracking has the *worst* object tracking. Full GRAIL trades 4.7 mm of body accuracy for +41.7 pp SR and halved object error.

Corroborating:
- **ResMimic**: base GMT alone → **10% SR** vs 92.5% with the residual. *"Although GMT alone yields slightly lower joint tracking error (due to its training objective), it performs poorly on object tracking."* And it documents the failure mode: approaching under a motion-tracking reward knocks the object over → ET → *"a local minimum where the robot retreats rather than engaging."*
- **Weave** names it a **gradient-scale** problem, not a reward-weight one: *"the reward couples body tracking, object tracking, and finger contact, whose gradients differ substantially in scale: an update dominated by the body tracking term yields a policy that follows the reference pose without closing fingers, a local optimum that early training falls into easily."* Fix: **Muon** optimizer on 2-D weight matrices (AdamW for biases) + SimBaV2 — and the optimizer contributed more than the architecture.
- **CoorDex** isolates the *action-space* half. Identical env, reward and PPO budget, walking G1, WalkGrab task: All-Joint-Space → **0% success / 0% grasp**; Body-Prior + Hand-Joint-Space → **0% / 1%** (and it learns to **stop walking** 90% of the time, converting loco-manipulation into stationary grasping); Monolithic Latent Residual → fails; **CoorDex → 55% success, 0% stop, 0% fall.** Their rationale: *"A hand prior that must also explain 6D wrist motion spends much of its capacity on body-side placement rather than on finger control."*
- **InterMimic's released G1 config is an unlabeled ablation.** Moving SMPL-X → `g1_29dof_with_hand`, the authors set `op: 5.0 → 0.0` — **world-frame object position reward disabled entirely** — while raising interaction-graph weight 5 → 20 and body rotation 1.5 → 10. Combined with object pose already being in the heading frame, the conclusion is: *on a real humanoid, don't reward world-frame object position; reward relative hand-object geometry.*

**Why so many works fix the base or float the wrist — their own words:**
- ManipTrans / DexMachina / DexTrack / Dexplore all bolt 6 virtual wrist DoF onto the hand. DexTrack: *"we additionally add three translation joints and three revolute joints to control the global position and orientation of the hand."* Dexplore: *"the wrist is modeled as a floating root joint in world coordinates."* Removes balance entirely; all reward capacity goes to object + fingers.
- ObjDex: *"Training RL policy that controls both robot arms and two multi-finger hands is possible in theory, but presents substantial challenges in practice due to the high degree of freedom of the robot action space."*
- **DexMan keeps the real G1 but freezes the lower body** (*"since tabletop tasks do not involve lower-body motion"*) and still beats floating-hand ManipTrans by +19 pp SR. Strongest evidence that *arm* kinematics aren't the problem — **balance** is.
- Isaac Lab's own shipped G1 manipulation envs set `fix_root_link=True` and `disable_gravity=True`.
- CoorDex on the field: *"existing humanoid loco-manipulation systems are often simplified into a stop-and-go process."*

**The Dex3-specific finding — the single most important number for Track 2.** CHORD §4.5, whole-body G1 + **Dex3 three-fingered hands**, references from 5-finger human hands:

| Category | n | CHORD (wrench-space) | Position Only (DexMachina-style) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rigid | 4 | 0.994 ± 0.008 | 0.460 ± 0.487 |
| Articulated | 4 | 0.914 ± 0.107 | **0.000 ± 0.000** |
| Multi-object | 4 | 0.866 ± 0.129 | 0.192 ± 0.166 |
| **Overall** | 12 | **0.925 ± 0.104** | **0.217 ± 0.333** |

*"Contact position rewards [are] tightly coupled with the demonstrated embodiment, whereas wrench space alignment guides learning independently of embodiment constraints."* For 5-finger → 3-finger transfer, this is the whole ballgame.

**Dexplore's RSE is the best idea for Tiers 2–3** (noisy references): two coupled schedules — (i) distance-modulated weights `w(D) = min(1, D/0.20 m)`, so kinematic matching weight is *proportional* to hand-object distance and the energy penalty *inversely* proportional (far → track the reference; near → let physics win); (ii) failure-rate-adaptive ET thresholds. Inspire/GRAB: **87.7% SR with RSE vs 29.9% without** — +48 to +58 pp, the largest single-design-choice gain in this survey.

### C4. Upper/lower decoupling — when it helps, when it hurts

| System | Approach | Position |
|---|---|---|
| **ExBody** [2402.16796] | Asymmetric reward: upper body imitates, **legs relieved of imitation**, only track commanded root velocity | The original "relax the legs" recipe |
| **ExBody2** [2412.13196] | *"decoupling the velocity tracking of the entire body from tracking body landmarks"*, teacher→student | Helps stability, costs exact global tracking |
| **HOMIE** [2502.13013] | Hard split: RL lower body on a pedal, isomorphic exoskeleton for arms, gloves for hands | Makes teleop tractable, forfeits coordination |
| **TWIST** [2505.02833] | **Against** — single whole-body controller from retargeted mocap | *"most current humanoid teleoperation systems… limit themselves to isolated locomotion or manipulation tasks"* |
| **CLONE** [2506.08931] | **Against** — MoE + closed-loop error correction | *"decouple upper- and lower-body control to maintain stability, restricting natural coordination, and operate open-loop… leading to accumulated drift"* |
| **OpenHLM** [2606.22174] | **Against** | *"yielding behaviors similar to those of a wheeled dual-arm platform."* Finds a joint-based whole-body teleop interface beats partial-DoF alternatives |
| **FALCON** [2505.06776] | **Dual-agent RL**, jointly trained: lower body = stability under external force, upper body = EE tracking with implicit force compensation | Best-quantified case *for* decoupling |
| **SkillBlender** [2506.09366] | Pretrain goal-conditioned primitives, then blend | *"minimal task-specific reward engineering… naturally regularizing behaviors to avoid reward hacking"* |
| **JAEGER** [2505.06584] | Two independent controllers, coarse (root velocity) + fine (joint angle) | *"alleviates the dimensionality curse and improves fault tolerance"* |

**FALCON's numbers** (G1, IsaacGym, 252 ACCAD targets), upper-body joint tracking error:

| Method | No-Force | Mid-Force | Large-Force |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monolithic WB-RL, no force curriculum | 0.46 | 1.03 | 1.65 |
| Monolithic WB-RL + force curriculum | 0.43 | 0.50 | 0.73 |
| FALCON, no force curriculum | **0.14** | 0.55 | 1.06 |
| **FALCON + force curriculum** | 0.21 | **0.24** | **0.37** |

FALCON's diagnosis of both alternatives: *"Lower-RL-Upper-IK approaches suffer from delayed force compensation… Monolithic-Whole-body-RL methods face sample inefficiency from coarsely related task objectives."* Real envelope 0–20 N payload, 0–100 N cart pull, 0–40 N door.

**The reconciliation.** Every "decoupling hurts" paper is about **teleop interfaces and expressiveness**, where a decoupled *interface* truncates the reachable behavior set. Every "decoupling helps" paper decouples in **learning structure** — separate agents, latent priors, residual layers — while training jointly in one environment with physics still coupled. CoorDex is the sharpest statement: decoupling into *frozen latent priors with a shared coordination trunk and separate residual heads* is what makes finger-level contact learnable at all on a walking humanoid; decoupling into *separate joint-space controllers* would not have been. **V2D's `JOINT_RESIDUAL` action on a frozen SONIC is exactly this second kind.**

**Carrying loads:** FALCON's torque-limit-aware force curriculum, and ResMimic's contact reward — whose ablation is instructive: without it the policy lifts a box with wrists alone, which succeeds in IsaacGym and **fails in MuJoCo and reality**; with it, coordinated torso+arm contact carries **4.5 kg on a G1 with a ~2.5 kg wrist limit**.

## D. Practical Isaac Lab specifics

### D1. How motion-tracking envs are actually structured

Converged pattern across BeyondMimic, Weave, HDMI and V2D:
- **Reference buffer** = a `Command` term, not an observation hack. BeyondMimic `MotionCommandCfg` (W&B-registry npz), Weave `env.commands.motion.motion_files` (npz list), V2D `TrackingCommand` reading a Hive-partitioned parquet (`whole_body/{dataset}/sequence_id={seq}/robot_name={robot}/data.parquet`, unified `motion_v1` schema: root pose, joint positions, EE targets, hand keypoints, finger joints, contacts, object trajectory, binary contact labels). The command term owns motion lookup, VOC decay, freeze windows, shoulder spread and action history. `SceneConfig.from_motion_file()` auto-discovers objects, support surfaces and episode length.
- **RSI**: on by default (`rsi: true` in Weave). GRAIL samples the start frame from the **first 30 frames**; HDMI resets "from a random frame with small random perturbations"; CHORD resets to arbitrary reference frames and **holds the command frozen for N frames** with VOC at scale 1.0 for M<N, then decays — and applies a **0.5 offset factor on G1 shoulder-yaw** to pull the arms back so the policy must re-approach.
- **Phase variable**: HDMI uses an explicit scalar ∈[0,1]. BeyondMimic / Weave / GRAIL instead feed **future-frame deltas over a horizon H** (GRAIL: 10 frames, 580-dim motion target block). The delta form generalizes better; the scalar phase is simpler for fixed-length clips.
- **Early termination**: anchor (pelvis) position + orientation, object position + orientation, EE (ankle/wrist) position, and **contact-loss-for-N-consecutive-frames**. Weave's exact set: pelvis 0.25 m, projected gravity 0.8, object pos 0.30 m, object gravity 0.3, EE 0.25 m, 10 lost contact frames. HDMI: 0.5 m / 1.2 rad across root, body-local and object. BeyondMimic uses **z-only** position terminations (0.25) — worth copying, it's much more forgiving of global drift.
- **Asymmetric actor/critic**: BeyondMimic's `policy` (noised) vs `critic` (clean + `robot_body_pos_b/ori_b`); GRAIL's critic gets privileged base linear velocity; Weave's critic gets privileged base lin-vel + current tracked-body poses.

### D2. RL libraries

`isaaclab_rl` ships **rsl_rl** (`RslRlVecEnvWrapper`), **skrl** (torch/jax/warp), **rl_games** (`RlGamesVecEnvWrapper`), **sb3**. **rsl_rl is the default for locomotion and what every relevant codebase uses** — BeyondMimic, Weave, V2D, unitree_rl_lab, WBC-AGILE, ResMimic.

- Asymmetric actor-critic: rsl_rl via `obs_groups={"actor":["policy"],"critic":["policy","privileged"]}`; rl_games via `"states"`; skrl via `state_space`; **sb3 does not support it** (`sb3.py:353`).

## E. The V2D Challenge itself

### E1. Structure

Three coupled tracks over shared task suites.

1. **Reconstruction** — third-person RGB → 4D HOI (human body, object pose + geometry, metric scale, consistent world frame, monocular, no depth). Scored on two equally weighted axes: *accuracy* (Chamfer distance to multi-view baseline human mesh; Chamfer to MV object mesh) and *physical plausibility* (joint acceleration error vs MV, object acceleration error vs MV, contact penetration vs MV). Script `eval_reconstruction.py`. Kit: `video_to_data/reconstruction`.
2. **Robotic Grounding (yours)** — third-person 4D HOI → robot policy. Framing question: *"How much reconstruction error can be tolerated?"* **Embodiment fixed and provided: "G1 + Dex3."** Three tiers: **Tier 1 (Clean)** multi-view caption baseline, the upper bound; **Tier 2 (Synthetic Corruption)** — *"jitter, dropout, and contact errors sampled from Track 1's error distributions"*; **Tier 3 (Real)** — off-the-shelf reconstructions, "today's best practice." Metrics: **"AUC, SP-SR, MP-SR, and MPPE as defined in the CHORD manuscript."** Script `eval_robotic_grounding.py`. Kit: `video_to_data/robotic_grounding`.
3. **Egocentric** — ego video → policy, method-agnostic ("explicit reconstruct-then-retarget or implicit end-to-end with pre-trained VLA or WAM"). Data "curated from our large-scale in-house dataset with held-out ground truth." Script `eval_e2e.py`.

**Timeline:** registration open now · **data release + official launch Sept 21, 2026** · leaderboard live to **Nov 4, 2026, 5:00 pm EST** · winners at **CoRL 2026, Nov 12**. ≤5 submissions/week, unlimited in the final three days. Awards: Track Winner, Runner-Up, and an Industry Innovation Award judged on *"deployability, data and compute efficiency, novelty, reproducibility"* — 9 total. **Eligibility requires a valid final submission, agreed code release under a research license, and a technical report.**

### E2. Metric definitions (from the CHORD PDF)

- **AUC** = the **DexMachina ADD-AUC**. Per articulated object *part*, compute ADD separately, average, then take area under the accuracy-threshold curve. **Not ADD-S** — exact correspondences exist. DexMachina's rationale: *"success rate reporting is based on how many timesteps… within given error thresholds, hence the results are highly sensitive to the threshold values (this case requires 3 different thresholds…)."*
- **SP-SR / MP-SR** = single-phase / multi-phase success rate, from SPIDER ([2511.09484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09484)) and ManipTrans respectively.
- **MPPE** = keypoint mean per-frame pose error (cm). Also reported: JPE (articulated joint position error, °), RPE (multi-object relative position error, cm).
- **Success**: a rollout succeeds if it completes "without object-centric termination, defined by position error above **15 cm** or rotation error above **40°**"; a *task* succeeds if completion ratio > **0.7**.

**CHORD's published bar** (60 tasks — 17 rigid, 24 articulated, 19 multi-object — 3 seeds, 4096 envs):

| Method | AUC↑ | SP-SR↑ | MP-SR↑ | JPE°↓ | RPE cm↓ | MPPE cm↓ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DexMachina | 0.737 | 0.690 | 0.569 | 13.10 | 9.92 | 15.88 |
| ManipTrans | 0.506 | 0.423 | 0.323 | 30.07 | 9.91 | 21.24 |
| SPIDER | 0.804 | 0.911 | 0.789 | 41.35 | 6.99 | 14.28 |
| Human2Sim2Robot | 0.730 | 0.686 | 0.569 | 11.80 | 9.78 | 16.59 |
| **CHORD** | **0.918** | **0.926** | **0.894** | **4.34** | **5.14** | **5.11** |

Reward-formulation ablation (same non-contact terms, only the contact supervision differs): CHORD 0.918 AUC → Position-Only 0.862 → No-Contact 0.774. Over 1,831 benchmark runs, CWS reward correlates with task success at **Pearson r = 0.798**. ADD-AUC stays **0.85–0.98 even at 40–48 s horizons**, where baselines collapse. Caveat the authors state themselves: *"object pose error is an imperfect evaluation metric: exact object placement may not be essential in some tasks, while a small pose error can lead to functional failure in others."*

`robotic_grounding/` supports **three embodiments: Sharpa Wave (dual floating hands), Dex3 (hands), G1 (whole body)**. Isaac Lab **2.3.1/2.3.2**, RSL-RL PPO, Docker `FROM nvcr.io/nvidia/isaac-lab:2.3.2` (needs `docker login nvcr.io` with an NGC API key).

Registered tasks:

| Task ID | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `Sharpa-V2D-v0` / `-Play` | floating-hand baseline |
| `SonicG1-v0` | base G1 env, `JOINT_RESIDUAL` action, unified obs, **all reward weights zeroed** — Hydra scaffold for custom reward configs |
| **`SonicG1-ReconHand-v0`** | **hand-accurate reference from the EE planner — your Track 2 task.** Residual scale **0.5** |
| `SonicG1-ReconHand-Stage{1,2,3}-v0` | staged recipe baked into the task |
| `SonicG1-ReconHand-EpisodeTimeout-v0` | fixed-length episodes |
| `SonicG1-ReconBody-v0` | body-accurate reference from third-person video (MHR). Residual scale **0.15**, force-closure reward |

Architecture: `V2DEnvCfg` (scene/commands/events/sim) → `G1SonicEnvCfg` (G1 + dex hands, SONIC action, unified obs, terminations, contact sensors, FrameTransformers) → `G1SonicReconHandEnvCfg` / `G1SonicReconBodyEnvCfg`. Key components: **TrackingCommand** (parquet loader, VOC decay, freeze periods, shoulder spread, action history), **SONIC Action** = `JOINT_RESIDUAL` (*"SONIC encodes reference trajectory, RL adds residuals after decode. Supports finger residuals."*), **Observations** (egocentric body-frame, 6D rotations throughout, future frame deltas, hand-object transforms, action history), **Rewards** (Gaussian-kernel tracking + contact/wrench; ReconBody uses force closure, ReconHand uses wrench-support matching), **Terminations** (freeze-aware for EE/object; anchor terminations always active).

Pipeline: MANO reference → `scripts/retarget/{arctic,taco}_to_dex3.py` → `robot_name=dex3` → `python -m robotic_grounding.planner.g1_planner --robot dex3 --v2d_start_at_first_contact --target_fps 100` → `robot_name=g1_dex3` + `reconstructed_stage/<seq>_support.usda` → training in place.

**The three-stage recipe** (`tasks/v2d_whole_body/EXAMPLE_SEQUENCES.md`), all `--num_envs 4096`:
1. **Stage 1 — no-collision warm-up** (5000 iters): robot↔object collisions **off** (support surfaces stay solid), **VOC always on**, hand-keypoint + finger-joint tracking rewards only, upper-body residuals, `--zero-actor` so residuals start at zero and the policy departs cleanly from the SONIC prior.
2. **Stage 2 — contact grounding** (50,000 iters): collisions **on**, VOC decays to zero on a fixed-timestep curriculum, contact rewards enabled (wrench-support + unintended/missed-contact). Resume from stage 1; **omit `--zero-actor`** or you wipe the resumed actor. Stage 2's contact rewards need `mano_{left,right}_link_contact_normals` in the loaded source.
3. **Stage 3 — full-sequence finetune** (5000 iters): full-length episodes (trajectory-end timeout), VOC off, curriculum disabled, **object-keypoint tracking weighted 10×**.

Sanity check for a smoke run: *"a non-zero `virtual_object_controller_scale_factor`, a climbing mean reward, and no missing-asset or PhysX-buffer errors."* **Always eval with the base `SonicG1-ReconHand-v0`** — the stage tasks bake training-time physics deltas (stage 1 has collisions off, stage 2 leaves the VOC partially driving the object) that make eval meaningless; checkpoints load unchanged in the base task.

**Pretrained checkpoints**: `huggingface-cli download nvidia/video-to-data-robotic-grounding-example` → `checkpoints/recon_hand/espresso_use_s09_seed42_model_59997.pt` (arctic `s09_espressomachine_use_02`, articulated) and `taco_empty_cup_bowl_seed42_model_59997.pt` (taco, rigid), plus three floating-hand ones. **You can eval on day one.** No `SonicG1-ReconBody-v0` checkpoint ships.

Three worked example sequences: `espresso` (arctic, articulated espresso machine), `taco_skim` (spoon + pan, rigid), `cup_bowl` (cup + bowl, rigid).

**CHORD's whole-body method**, from the appendix — this is what the residual actually does: residual on the frozen SONIC WBC with **action scale 0.5 for non-finger joints, 0.15 for fingers**; observations add whole-body joint state + anchor/EE/joint-position target deltas over N future reference frames, and **contact observations are removed** from the whole-body observation space; terminations add anchor position + orientation error; reset holds the reference frozen for N frames with VOC at 1.0 for M<N, with a **0.5 offset factor on G1 shoulder-yaw** pulling the arms back so the policy must learn to recover toward the object.

**Related NVIDIA datasets** (all CC-BY-4.0 unless noted): `nvidia/video-to-data-robot-dexterity-task-library-and-dataset` (924.8 GB, 1,215 motion sequences, 121,500 robot episodes, Parquet + LeRobot v3, TACO-derived, Sharpa hands) · `nvidia/video-to-data-object-assets` v0.3 (69 objects / 168 packages, GLB + USD, meter-scale Z-up with authored collision, mass, inertia — *"not measured physical ground truth"*) · `nvidia/form-hoi` · `nvidia/PhysicalAI-Robotics-Locomanipulation-GRAIL` (CC-BY-NC-4.0).

**Discrepancy worth knowing:** the CHORD paper reports FlashSAC with 2048 envs; the public repo ships **rsl_rl PPO** with 4096. The "~2 h/sequence on one L40S" figure belongs to the paper's SAC setup — budget considerably more for the public PPO path, especially stage 2's 50k iterations.

### Six-week shape

- **Wk 1** (now–Sept 25): environment up, reproduce both released checkpoints, read the CHORD PDF end to end, register. Pull challenge data Sept 21.
- **Wk 2**: reproduce the three-stage recipe on one Tier-1 sequence. **Verify your ADD-AUC matches theirs on the released checkpoint before anything else.**
- **Wk 3–4**: two highest-EV deltas — (a) object observations *and* rewards strictly in the robot root frame (GRAIL: +23.5 pp); (b) **keep the wrench-space contact reward, do not substitute a position-matched one** — the 5-finger→3-finger gap is exactly where position rewards collapse (0.925 → 0.217). Then try ResMimic's point-cloud object reward as a stage-3 replacement, since it's the reward most directly aligned with ADD.
- **Wk 5**: Tiers 2–3. Dexplore's RSE is the highest-leverage published idea for noisy references (+48–58 pp): distance-modulated reward weights `w(D) = min(1, D/0.20 m)` plus failure-rate-adaptive ET thresholds. That is precisely the Tier-2/3 corruption problem, and nothing in the released V2D recipe does it.
- **Wk 6**: seed sweeps, per-sequence specialists, submission packaging, tech report.

One structural note: **per-sequence specialist policies are the norm in every strong result here** — CHORD, DexMachina and BeyondMimic all train one policy per sequence, and CHORD explicitly frames its result as *"training recipe scalability across heterogeneous tasks, rather than cross-task policy generalization."* Unless the leaderboard rules forbid it, don't burn week 3 chasing a single generalist policy. Weave is the one counterexample — a joint 9-object policy beat nine specialists 95.3% vs 91.5% at matched total iterations — so it's worth one cheap experiment, not a strategy.
