### 1. HOI trajectory denoising/refinement with learned priors

**TOCH** (Zhou, Bhatnagar, Lenssen, Pons-Moll, ECCV 2022). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07982 · Code: https://github.com/kzhou23/toch
- Representation: "TOCH fields" — point-wise, object-centric encoding of hand position relative to the object surface; a temporal denoising autoencoder cleans the field sequence, then MANO pose is re-fit to match.
- **Scope: hand only.** Per the abstract, fields "encode the hand position relative to the object" — the object trajectory is the reference frame, not something TOCH itself corrects. If your object 6-DoF stream is noisy, TOCH doesn't touch it.
- Trained on GRAB (SMPL-X + MANO + object mocap). Trajectory-level (temporal autoencoder), not per-frame.
- License: no LICENSE file found in the repo — flag as unverified/possibly missing, confirm before use.
- Integration cost: low-medium. Well-scoped model; main cost is writing your own data loader (their "custom dataset" path is marked TODO).

**GeneOH Diffusion** (Liu & Yi, ICLR 2024). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14810 · Code: https://github.com/Meowuu7/GeneOH-Diffusion · **License: MIT**
- The strongest hit overall: a diffusion denoiser that cleans **both hand and object jointly**, trained once on GRAB and evaluated zero-shot on GRAB(perturbed), HOI4D (rigid + articulated), and TACO — i.e. already benchmarked across the kind of clean/synthetic-corrupted/cross-domain spread your three noise tiers represent.
- Full train+inference code, checkpoints, preprocessing scripts released — most complete package in this set.
- Integration cost: low. Best single candidate for "repair before retargeter." Main risk: HOI4D/GRAB contact/articulation conventions may not map 1:1 onto your multi-object/articulated format.

**InterDiff** (Xu et al., ICCV 2023). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16905 · Code: https://github.com/Sirui-Xu/InterDiff · **License: MIT**
- Two-stage: interaction diffusion + a physics-informed **correction module** inside the sampling loop (penetration/floating-contact fixing) — but framed as forecasting/generating future interaction, not denoising an already-complete noisy sequence. Full-body SMPL + object (BEHAVE, HO-GCN), not MANO-native.
- Trajectory-level. Integration cost: medium-high — real repurposing needed (SMPL→MANO, forecasting→denoising).

**G-HOP** (Ye, Gupta, Kitani, Tulsiani, 2024). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12383. Diffusion prior jointly over object+MANO hand, 7 datasets/155 categories. **Code status not verified this session** — flag before relying on it.

**Physically Plausible Full-Body HOI Synthesis** (Braun, Christen, Kocabas, Aksan, Hilliges, 2023). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07907. RL+physics skill priors. **Code status not verified this session.**

No 2025-2026 paper surpassed GeneOH Diffusion as a code-complete, MIT-licensed object+hand HOI *denoiser* in this pass.

### 2. Contact estimation and refinement from noisy HOI

**ContactOpt** (Grady et al., CVPR 2021). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07267 · Code: https://github.com/facebookresearch/ContactOpt · **License: MIT**
- Learned contact-prediction network → differentiable optimization moves MANO pose to satisfy predicted contact. Hybrid: re-derives contact from a model conditioned on geometry, not pure geometry, and not pure label-trusting either. Trained on perturbed ContactPose. **Single-frame only**, no built-in temporal handling.

**GraspTTA** ("Hand-Object Contact Consistency Reasoning for Human Grasps Generation," Jiang, Liu, Wang, Wang, ICCV 2021). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03304 · Code: https://github.com/hwjiang1510/GraspTTA (license not confirmed — check before use)
- Clearest "**re-derive contact from geometry, don't trust labels**" method here: test-time optimization enforces consistency between hand-contact and object-contact fields regardless of initial labels. Trained on ObMan, tested out-of-domain on HO-3D. Single-frame. Integration cost low-medium (2021-era code, may need dependency updates).

**Differentiable Force Closure (DFC)** (Liu, Liu, Jiao, Zhu, Zhu, RA-L 2021/2022). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09194
- Pure physics route: differentiable force-closure estimator optimizes grasp pose directly from geometry, no learned contact labels at all, explicitly hand-structure-agnostic (relevant given the eventual Dex3 gap). **Could not locate a working public code repo this session** (a plausible guess 404'd) — flag clearly, verify code exists before counting on it.

**DECO** (Tripathi et al., ICCV 2023 Oral). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15273 · Code: https://github.com/sha2nkt/deco · **License: non-commercial research only** (MPI license, confirmed — explicitly forbids commercial/production use).
- **Scope mismatch**: human-scene contact (full SMPL body vs. environment), not hand-object; single image, not sequence. Commonly cited but wouldn't fit without retraining on hand-object data. Not recommended for a 6-week window.

**HOLD** (Fan et al., CVPR 2024 Highlight). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18448 · Code: https://github.com/zc-alexfan/hold · **License: MIT**
- Category-agnostic joint hand+object reconstruction from monocular video (neural volumetric rendering), no pre-scanned templates needed. Whether it explicitly outputs discrete contact labels (vs. just consistent geometry) **could not be confirmed from the README** — flag as unverified. More relevant to your tier-3 (monocular reconstruction) noise source than as a contact refiner. Video/trajectory-level; integration cost medium-high (full per-sequence optimization pipeline, not a lightweight pass).

**Bottom line:** GraspTTA and DFC genuinely re-derive contact from geometry; ContactOpt is a learned-target hybrid; DECO/HOLD are commonly-cited but don't fit your hand-object/sequence setting without real rework.

### 3. Physics-plausibility projection of object trajectories

No standalone, general-purpose "take one bad object trajectory, output a physically-consistent one" open-source tool turned up. What exists instead:

**D-Grasp** (Christen, Kocabas, Aksan, Hwangbo, Song, Hilliges, CVPR 2022). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03028 · Code: https://github.com/christsa/dgrasp (license not confirmed)
- RL policy in physics sim, grasps/moves an object to a target 6-DoF pose while staying physically valid. A *generator conditioned on a target*, not a *projector of a given trajectory* — right mechanism, wrong entry point without retraining. Integration cost medium-high (own sim + RL training per use case).

**InterMimic** (Xu, Ling, Wang, Gui, CVPR 2025). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20390 · Code: https://github.com/Sirui-Xu/InterMimic · **License: MIT**
- **Flag as most architecturally relevant hit in the whole report**: physics-based whole-body HOI imitation, and the released code explicitly includes **Unitree G1 support**, teacher/student distillation, full train+inference. Tracking a noisy kinematic reference in Isaac Gym/Lab yields a rollout that's physically consistent by construction (no interpenetration, grounded contact) — conceptually exactly the "projection" operator you want, and it already targets your downstream robot.
- Caveat: **no documentation found stating it's designed to consume noisy/corrupted references** — it's built to imitate given mocap-quality HOI data, so using it as a denoiser is a legitimate but nontrivial reframing. Trajectory-level by definition. Integration cost: high if training new policies per object/skill in 6 weeks; low-medium if you can reuse their released G1 pipeline and only swap in your own reference trajectories for tracking.

**PhysHOI** (Wang, Lin, Zeng, Luo, Zhang, Zhang, 2023). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04393 · Code: https://github.com/wyhuai/PhysHOI (LICENSE.txt present, terms not confirmed)
- Same family (contact-graph imitation reward, no hand-crafted per-task reward) but released checkpoints are scoped to basketball (BallPlay) — not general-object out of the box. Isaac Gym + 6GB+ GPU. Useful mainly as an architectural template.

**PhysDiff** (Yuan, Song, Iqbal, Vahdat, Kautz, ICCV 2023 Oral, NVIDIA). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.02500
- Physics-projection-inside-diffusion-sampling for human motion (not object). **No public code found** (github.com/NVlabs/PhysDiff → 404). Worth reading as a conceptual template, but you'd reimplement it.

**SE(3)/Lie-group temporal filtering in practice** (general robotics-estimation practice, not one paper):
- **GTSAM** — https://github.com/borglab/gtsam, **BSD license** confirmed — factor-graph smoothing, the standard tool for proper manifold-consistent pose-sequence smoothing.
- **UKF-M** — https://github.com/CAOR-MINES-ParisTech/ukfm, **BSD-3-Clause** confirmed — unscented KF formulated natively on Lie groups, lighter-weight than GTSAM.
- Common cheap baseline (not a library): filter translation and rotation separately — spline/Savitzky-Golay on translation, quaternion SLERP-based smoothing on rotation. Not strictly manifold-consistent but still widely shipped for jitter removal; won't fix gaps or gross errors.
- **This is probably your cheapest, highest-certainty win across all five topics** — days, not weeks, to wire an SE(3) smoother onto the object stream. It only fixes jitter, not interpenetration or gross failures.

**Realistic 6-week plan for this topic:** cheap SE(3) filtering (GTSAM/UKF-M) always-on for jitter, plus either InterMimic's G1-aware physics-tracking for gross-error correction (if RL training budget allows) or a hand-written contact-based post-hoc correction (de-penetrate against the topic-2 contact labels) as the fallback.

### 4. Temporal infilling for dropout

Thin on purpose-built HOI-specific solutions; closest fits:

**CHOIS** (Li, Clegg, Mottaghi, Wu, Puig, Liu, ECCV 2024). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03913 · Code: https://github.com/lijiaman/chois_release · **License: MIT**
- Conditional diffusion generating synchronized human+object motion from language, initial state, and **sparse waypoints** — already solves "fill in dense trajectory given sparse known frames" for both streams jointly, even though framed as language-conditioned generation rather than gap-filling. No physics post-processing step found in the repo. Trajectory-level, tested on OMOMO (seen) and 3D-FUTURE (unseen objects). **Best-positioned adoptable building block for this topic** — repurpose the waypoint-conditioning path (frames flanking a gap = waypoints); likely needs retraining/fine-tuning on your object set.

**CondMDI** (Cohan, Tevet, Reda, Peng, van de Panne, SIGGRAPH 2024). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11126 · Code: https://github.com/setarehc/diffusion-motion-inbetweening · **License: MIT** (CLIP/SMPL/PyTorch3D deps carry their own licenses)
- General diffusion in-betweening, dense-or-sparse keyframe conditioning. **Human body only** (HumanML3D/SMPL) — no object, no MANO specifically. Could not confirm arbitrary 5-50 frame gap support out of the box vs. needing retraining at your target gap distribution — flag for your own testing. Integration cost medium-high (SMPL→MANO+object adaptation needed).

**Robust Motion In-betweening** (Harvey, Yurick, Nowrouzezahrai, Pal, Ubisoft, SIGGRAPH 2020/TOG). No official code release found; only an unofficial reimplementation: https://github.com/xjwxjw/Pytorch-Robust-Motion-In-betweening (license unclear). Foundational named method, still widely cited, but **not a safe 6-week bet** given unofficial code status and human-body-only scope.

**OMOMO** (Li, Wu, Liu, SIGGRAPH Asia 2023). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16237 · Code: https://github.com/lijiaman/omomo_release · **License: MIT**
- Requires a **complete** object motion sequence as conditioning — not built for infilling gaps in the object stream itself. Listed because CHOIS builds on it and shares data format/checkpoints.

**Bottom line:** no purpose-built "HOI dropout infiller" with public code evaluated on 5-50-frame gaps exists. CHOIS is the best adoptable piece. For short gaps, benchmark classical SLERP+spline interpolation followed by a GeneOH Diffusion/TOCH cleanup pass (topic 1) before investing in a learned infiller.

### 5. Human-to-robot hand retargeting, MANO → 3-finger Dex3

**dex-retargeting** (Yuzhe Qin et al., AnyTeleop project). Code: https://github.com/dexsuite/dex-retargeting · **License: MIT**
- Three formulations: **vector retargeting** (video-only human hand), **position retargeting** (matches absolute fingertip/joint positions — closer fit to your MANO-keypoint input), and a **DexPilot-style optimizer**. Robot models via companion `dex-urdf` repo; library itself is URDF-driven/robot-agnostic.
- Confirmed empirically: Unitree's own official teleop repo, **avp_teleoperate** (https://github.com/unitreerobotics/avp_teleoperate), wraps dex-retargeting specifically to drive the **Dex3-1** hand — so Dex3 support exists via this integration, not a config shipped in dex-retargeting itself.
- Supports both per-frame and sequence retargeting (`SeqRetargeting` class). **Integration cost: low** — the single easiest 6-week win in this whole report: MIT, already production-used for Dex3-1, well documented.

**DexPilot** (Handa, Van Wyk, Yang, Liang, Chao, Wan, Birchfield, Ratliff, Fox — NVIDIA, ICRA 2020). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03135
- Vision-based teleop for a 23-DoF hand-arm system; its task-space keypoint-matching + self-collision-avoidance formulation is what dex-retargeting's "DexPilot" mode implements. No independent standalone code release found — dex-retargeting is the practical way to use this today.

**AnyTeleop** (Qin, Yang, Huang, Van Wyk, Su, Wang, Chao, Fox, RSS 2023). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04577 · Project page: https://yzqin.github.io/anyteleop/ · Retargeting code: https://github.com/dexsuite/dex-retargeting (same repo; web visualizer separately at https://github.com/NVlabs/sim-web-visualizer)
- Unifies retargeting across heterogeneous arms/hands/cameras — reference architecture for handling morphology diversity in one codebase, though I found no explicit written discussion of 3-finger-specific failure modes on the project page.

**ManipTrans** (Li, Li, Liu, Li, Huang, CVPR 2025). arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21860 · Code: https://github.com/ManipTrans/ManipTrans · **License: GPL v3**
- Clearest **function/contact-based alternative to pure kinematic retargeting**: pretrain a general MANO-trajectory-imitation policy, then **residual-RL fine-tune** per target hand against physics/contact objectives (their DexManipNet, 3.3K episodes). Supports Inspire, Shadow, Allegro, XHand, Arti-Mano — multi-finger hands, **not verified against a 3-finger hand**, but the mechanism (configurable `hand2dex_mapping` + per-joint `weight_idx`, no literal joint correspondence required) is exactly the right shape for a severe DoF mismatch like Dex3. Training+inference code released. Integration cost medium-high for 6 weeks (GPL v3 may itself be a licensing blocker; residual-RL stage needs per-hand physics-sim training).

**What specifically breaks with only 3 fingers** (synthesis from the above sources plus general domain knowledge — flagged as reasoning, not a single citation):
- Vector/position/DexPilot-style optimizers match *correspondence pairs* of keypoints. MANO gives 5 fingertips, Dex3 gives 3 — there's no natural target for the two dropped (typically ring+pinky): either discard their targets (losing information your contact-label stream is providing) or fold them into the nearest kept finger (distorting its tracked pose).
- Grasp types relying on the missing fingers for stability (power/cylindrical wraps, ulnar-side pinches) have no faithful 3-finger equivalent — kinematic keypoint-matching alone produces a pose that looks close but has degraded force/contact closure, which is exactly the gap ManipTrans's residual-RL and DFC's force-closure objective (topic 2) are meant to close.
- Self-collision/finger-proximity terms in DexPilot-style optimizers are hand-specific tuning, not just a reusable 5-finger config, when finger count/spacing changes this much.
- At 7 total DoF/hand, in-hand finger-gaiting implied by a MANO trajectory is frequently unrealizable outright — the reference itself needs simplifying to achievable Dex3 contact patterns before retargeting, not left for the RL stage to fix from a naive 5-finger retarget. This is the strongest argument for doing contact-aware repair (topics 2+3) **before** the retargeter, matching your working thesis directly: bad 5-finger contact intent in means either broken 3-finger contact out, or a much harder job for downstream RL to invent contact from scratch.
