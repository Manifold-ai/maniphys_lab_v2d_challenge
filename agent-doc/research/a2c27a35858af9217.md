### Three tracks (not two)

**Track 1 — Reconstruction.** Third-person monocular RGB → 4D HOI. One RGB camera, third person, no rigs, no depth. Recover human body + object pose + geometry in **metric scale** in a consistent world frame. Data is NVIDIA's in-house third-person RGB set with held-out GT; explicitly includes heavy occlusion, bimanual coordination, long horizons. Submission via `eval_reconstruction.py`.
Evaluation is **two equally-weighted axes against a multi-view (MV) reconstruction upper bound**:
- *Accuracy*: Chamfer distance to MV human mesh; Chamfer distance to MV object mesh.
- *Physical plausibility*: joint acceleration error vs MV; object acceleration error vs MV; contact penetration vs MV.
Starter toolkit: https://github.com/nvidia-isaac/video_to_data/tree/release/0.2.0/reconstruction

**Track 2 — Robotic Grounding.** 4D HOI → policy. Embodiment and simulator are **fixed: Unitree G1 + Dex3** (so you compete on retargeting + policy learning, not sim engineering). Three noise tiers:
- Tier 1: clean multi-view capture (upper bound)
- Tier 2: **synthetic corruption — jitter, dropout, contact errors sampled from Track 1's measured error distributions**
- Tier 3: off-the-shelf reconstructions (today's best practice)
Metrics: **AUC, SP-SR, MP-SR, MPPE as defined in the CHORD manuscript.** Harder tiers weighted more. Submission via `eval_robotic_grounding.py`.
Starter toolkit: https://github.com/nvidia-isaac/video_to_data/tree/release/0.2.0/robotic_grounding

**Track 3 — Egocentric.** Egocentric video → policy, end-to-end, method-agnostic (explicit reconstruct-then-retarget *or* implicit VLA/WAM). Submission via `eval_e2e.py`.

### On "ADD-AUC" specifically
The challenge page says "AUC," not "ADD-AUC." The chain is: CHORD states "We adopted standardized metrics (**AUC from DexMachina**, SP-SR from SPIDER, MP-SR from ManipTrans)," plus functional continuous errors **JPE** (articulated joint position error, °), **RPE** (multi-object relative position error, cm), **MPPE** (keypoint mean per-frame pose error, cm). CHORD's figures then explicitly call it "**DexMachina ADD-AUC**."

DexMachina (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24853, Appendix B) defines it: ADD = Average Distance of model points, AUC = area under the accuracy-threshold curve. Crucially they **compute ADD per object part separately (to handle articulated objects) and average ADDs before computing AUC** — deliberately *not* ADD-S, because the demonstration gives exact matching targets. CHORD reports near-saturated ADD-AUC ≈ 0.85–0.98 even on 40–48 s horizons; and when grounding from *real noisy video*, it reports **0.647 AUC** — that's your realistic Tier-3 reference point.

# TOPIC 1 — Prior challenges: noisy perception → learned policy, and what actually won

Ordered roughly by transferability to V2D.

## A. The closest analogue: HANDS @ ICCV 2025 "Dexterous HO Tracker" Challenge
Literally V2D Track 2 in miniature: transfer human MoCap HOI to dexterous hands in Isaac Gym; single-hand, bimanual-one-object, bimanual-two-object (pen + cap). Datasets OakInk2 (**described by organizers as "noisy kinematic data"**) and GigaHand. Baseline = **ManipTrans** (https://maniptrans.github.io); you must beat it to be valid. Eval on a **withheld, augmented** set "similar but not identical" to released eval sequences.
- Challenge page: https://hands-workshop.org/challenges/tracker2025.html
- Track list: https://hands-workshop.org/challenge2025.html

**Winner (1st): Team HCB-Hand (HUST + ByteDance/PICO + A*STAR).** Report: https://hands-workshop.org/files/2025/HCB-Hand.pdf
Concrete trick: keep ManipTrans's two-stage structure (frozen pre-trained generalist *hand imitator* that is explicitly "resilient to noise," then a residual policy on joint hand-object obs), but **replace the single residual policy with a Mixture of Residual Experts (MoRE)**: 4 sparse experts + 1 shared expert, router over encoded observations, top-K = 1. Rationale: one net can't cover the full spectrum of noisy observations; route to specialists. Result: **76.2% single-hand, 52.8% bimanual — 1st place.**

**Takeaway for V2D Track 2 Tier 2/3:** the winning move was *capacity routing conditioned on observation*, not better filtering. Noise-induced multimodality in the reference → mixture of experts.

## B. HANDS 2024 Multiview Egocentric Hand Tracking (MegoTrack) — closest analogue to V2D Track 1
Task: hand pose/shape from egocentric multi-view; UmeTrack + HOT3D; metric MPJPE. Leaderboard: https://eval.ai/web/challenges/challenge-page/2333/overview

**Team JVhands (Jiiov Technology)** — https://hands-workshop.org/files/2024/JVHANDS.pdf — **13.92 mm MPJPE on UmeTrack, 21.66 mm on HOT3D.** The three tricks are exactly the class the V2D Track 1 organizers will reward:
1. **Crop jittering + extrinsic-parameter noise augmentation** — random noise in (−0.5, 0.5) added to the *translation component of camera extrinsics*, plus FOV/rotation perturbation of the perspective crop, explicitly to stop overfitting to a fixed camera layout. (They diagnosed a real distribution gap in extrinsics translation between UmeTrack and HOT3D.)
2. **Architectural noise isolation**: split the FTL feature-lifting module into **translation-invariant** features (→ shape, pose, global orientation) and **translation-variant** features (→ position). Only the part that *must* depend on extrinsics does.
3. **"Neural Smooth" offline post-processing**: treat per-frame predictions as *trainable parameters* over a sequence and optimize with a **2D-projection loss + acceleration loss**. Motivation: 2D landmarks generalize better than 3D, so use them to correct 3D. This is exactly V2D Track 1's "physical plausibility" axis (joint/object acceleration error) being optimized directly at test time.

Standard aug stack also present: random brightness/contrast, blur, Gaussian noise, left→right flipping to unify hand chirality.

**Team ACE, ARCTIC 2nd place** — https://hands-workshop.org/files/2024/ACE.pdf — bimanual category-agnostic HOI reconstruction, **+12.6% on CD_h** over HOLD baseline. Three fixes, all noise-handling:
- Replace SAM-Track with **SAM-2** for masks (SAM-Track confused left/right hands and collapsed under heavy occlusion).
- **Relation-aware Two-Hand Tokenization (RAT)** grafted onto HaMeR: R-CNN boxes → relative-distance and overlap maps → MLP → relation tokens concatenated with ViT vision tokens → self-attention → MANO decoder. Fixes the fact that HaMeR estimates each hand independently.
- **Self-Boost MANO registration**: HOLD linearly interpolates frames where R-CNN fails to detect an occluded hand, which compounds error over consecutive misses. Instead, seed the current frame with the *stored 3D vertices from the previous frame*, then **accept only if a loss threshold is met**, and only then update the stored vertices. A validity-gated temporal carry-forward, not blind interpolation.

Related 2026 edition (ECCV, now "DexHAND"): https://hands-workshop.org/workshop2026.html — DexGrasp Motion 1st place = "**A Residual Mixture-of-Experts Policy for Underactuated Dexterous Grasping**" (Gong, Lu, Qin, Luo, Ding, Chen). MoE-over-residual-policy wins again. Challenge rules: https://hands-workshop.org/challenge2026.html

## C. Real Robot Challenge (TriFinger) — the canonical "noisy pose estimate in the loop" lesson
- RRC 2020/2021 report: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v176/bauer22a.html
- RRC 2022 report: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v220/gurtler23a.html · arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07741

**The noise:** object pose came from a 3-camera vision tracker. Organizers themselves note it was "unreliable with respect to rotation around the long axis" of the narrow cuboid — so they *changed the reward definition* to only use the angle between long axes. In RRC 2022 they write that the Lift task "together with the noise on the pose estimation of the cube" is "quite unforgiving."

**RRC 2022 winners (offline RL from mixed-quality real robot data):**
- **1st, "excludedrice" (Wang & McCarthy, UCD)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13019. Key insight: plain BC on expert data beats fancy offline RL; plain BC on *mixed* data is bad because of action multimodality. So: **iterative semi-supervised expert-data filtering** — take the top-scoring slice, train a NN on it, use it as a binary classifier to pull more expert data out, repeat until the recovered expert set stops growing. Plus **rotational-symmetry data augmentation** exploiting TriFinger's 3-fold symmetry, with a correction paradigm because manufacturing asymmetries mean the mathematically-augmented data is off-distribution. **Only team to exceed the behavior policies' own score.**
- **2nd, "decimalcurlew" (POSTECH/ETRI)** — TD3+BC with state/action normalization and action rescaling to [−1,1], plus **an added spatial action-smoothness regularizer** (Mysore et al.) so nearby states produce similar actions — chosen *explicitly because of observation noise on the real robot*.
- **3rd (shared), "superiordinosaur"** — the most directly reusable trick: observed the vision tracker suffers **(i) high delay and (ii) noisy cube pose**. Solution: a **validity check + moving average** — for each new pose, check whether *delay < threshold* or *confidence > threshold*; if yes update a running average of the cube pose, if no **hold the last pose**. Plus supervised learning with early stopping. They report TD3+BC and LSTM architectures both underperformed this.
- **3rd (shared), "jealousjaguar" (ETH Zurich)** — IQL, to avoid OOD action queries.

**Earlier RRC sim2real:** Allshire et al., "Transferring Dexterous Manipulation from GPU Simulation to a Remote Real-World TriFinger," https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.09779 — **keypoint-based pose representation instead of position+quaternion** (in both observations *and* reward) + domain randomization → **83% success** on the remote real robot. The keypoint trick is the standard fix for quaternion discontinuity under noisy pose and is what CHORD/DexMachina also use.

**Organizers' own takeaway (2020 vs 2021):** in 2020 **hand-crafted state machines + motion primitives beat learning**. In 2021 pure RL (DDPG+HER, team thriftysnipe) won stage 1 while the classical extension (decimalswift) was runner-up — and the comparison is instructive: the classical policy grips stably and rarely drops but moves slowly; the RL policy drops more often **but recovers very fast**, i.e. it learned that speed-with-recovery beats caution. Stage 2 (rearrange 25 dice) was solved by nobody.

## D. Habitat Challenges — noisy depth, no odometry
- 2020 (the noisy one): https://aihabitat.org/challenge/2020/ — no GPS+Compass for PointNav, **LoCoBot-benchmarked actuation noise (PyRobot)**, RGB and depth sensor noise, sliding-on-collision disabled.
- 2021: https://aihabitat.org/challenge/2021/ · 2022: https://aihabitat.org/challenge/2022/ · 2023: https://aihabitat.org/challenge/2023/

**PointNav-under-noise winners and the trick that mattered — learn visual odometry as a drop-in GPS replacement:**
- 2020 1st: **Occupancy Anticipation** (Ramakrishnan, Al-Halah, Grauman), SPL 0.21 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.09285
- 2020 2nd: **Ego-Localization** (Datta, Maksymets, Hoffman, Lee, Batra, Parikh), SPL 0.15 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03231
- **Differentiable SLAM-net** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07593 — took Habitat 2020 PointNav from 37% → **64% success**; "significantly outperforms ORB-SLAM in noisy conditions."
- 2021 1st: inspir.ai robotics (SPL 0.74); 2nd: UCU MLab "Robust Visual Odometry" (SPL 0.72).
- The distilled recipe: **Partsey et al., "The Surprising Effectiveness of Visual Odometry Techniques for Embodied PointGoal Navigation"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11550. Concretely: (1) **geometric-invariance self-supervision** — predicting (I_t, I_{t+1}) and the reversed pair must satisfy θ^{t→t+1} + θ^{t+1→t} = 0 etc.; (2) **dropout-as-ensemble** on the last two FC layers with inference-time parameter scaling — same benefit as 10-pass averaging at **118.8 FPS vs 8.45 FPS**; (3) **depth discretization into one-hot bins**, which raised noise tolerance ~50%; (4) **separate action-specific models** for forward/turn-left/turn-right to handle class imbalance; (5) soft top-down orthographic projection of depth. Result: 82% success / 63% SPL with learned VO vs 97%/71% with GT GPS, and the challenge metric went 64.5% → 71.7%.

**ObjectNav with noisy semantic segmentation — auxiliary tasks.** Habitat 2021 ObjectNav 1st place "Red Rabbit": **Ye et al., "Auxiliary Tasks and Exploration Enable ObjectGoal Navigation,"** https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04112. Six auxiliary tasks on independent GRUs: CPC|A at k∈{4,16}, PBL at k=8, Action Distribution Prediction, Generalized Inverse Dynamics, Coverage Prediction. Their own analysis says the mechanism is **dimensionality reduction of the recurrent dynamics** — fixed-point analysis shows smaller memories, constraining the RNN to low-dimensional trajectories, and this matters *specifically because RedNet semantic segmentation is noisy at eval*. Plus a **tethered two-head policy** (shared trunk, different reward per head) to decouple the dense coverage reward from the sparse success reward.
Also relevant: **Stubborn** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07359) — multi-scale collision mapping specifically because "inaccurate depth observation" traps the agent; and **SemExp** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00643), 2020 ObjectNav winner at 25.3% vs 18.8% for 2nd.

Habitat 2022 ObjectNav (GPS+Compass noiseless, no injected noise): 1st **ByteBOT** SPL 0.35 "Integrating Map-Based Method with End-to-End Learning"; 2nd **BadSeed/IRLNav** SPL 0.33; 3rd **Walle** (Scene Memory Transformer + CLIP) SPL 0.30 — slides linked from the 2022 page.

## E. HomeRobot OVMM Challenge (NeurIPS 2023) — the "just fix the failure modes" school
- Challenge: https://aihabitat.org/challenge/2023_homerobot_ovmm/ · benchmark paper https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11565 · code https://github.com/facebookresearch/home-robot
- Results: **1st UniTeam** 10.8% sim / **50% real**; 2nd Rulai 3.2% / 0%; 3rd KuzHum 2.4% / 0%.

**UniTeam (1st), https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08611** — an "improved baseline," and the improvement list is a catalogue of noisy-perception patches:
- **Per-category dynamic confidence thresholds** instead of one global threshold (object-specific and receptacle-specific).
- **Height-based depth filtering** to kill floor-level false-positive receptacles.
- Store **detection probabilities in the BEV map**, then pick the *reachable* receptacle with highest matching probability (not the nearest detection).
- Fold detected **collisions back into the obstacle map** at the last short-term goal.
- **Frontier-vs-goal arbitration** to break infinite oscillation loops.
- **Rotate left/right and retry** when the first pick fails (expands FOV); **explicit pick-success verification** that reverts to the navigation phase on failure.
- Placing: incremental small-step approach for accurate distance, **edge safety buffer**, surface fallback when the receptacle isn't detectable, raised drop height for large objects.

**KuzHum (3rd), https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12048** — better semantic segmentation module + better place-skill policy.

## F. BEHAVIOR-1K Challenge (NeurIPS 2025) — the current state of the art on "noisy long-horizon VLA"
Both top reports are gold for V2D Track 3.

**1st place, https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06951** (blog: https://robot-learning-collective.github.io/winning-behavior-1k-challenge.html). q-score **0.26** public and private (0.2605 / 0.2599), on π₀.₅ (SigLIP + Gemma). Tricks, in rough order of impact:
- **Gripper recovery rule**: if the gripper is closed and it was *never* closed in the training data for this task at this stage, treat it as a failed grasp and fully reopen. This alone **roughly doubled success on 13 tasks**. (A cheap, brutally effective test-time correction rule.)
- **Correlated noise for flow matching**: ε ~ 𝒩(0, βΣ + (1−β)I), β=0.5, Σ = empirical action covariance — because robot actions are temporally and cross-dimensionally correlated.
- **Correlation-aware inpainting** across rolling windows: execute 26 of 30 predicted actions, carry 4, and propagate corrections through M_corr = Σ_UO Σ_OO⁻¹ rather than treating inpainted and free actions independently.
- **System-2 stage tracking**: 5–15 stages per task, linear classifier on VLM features, ~99% train accuracy, with **majority voting over the 3 most recent predictions** to stop noisy predictions from triggering premature stage transitions. Directly targets non-Markovian visual ambiguity.
- **Multi-sample flow matching**: 15 flow samples per single VLM forward pass to cut gradient variance while amortizing the expensive vision-language compute.
- **Custom hierarchical attention masks** that *isolate reliable tokens from noisy ones* — images and task embeddings are deterministic from observations, so they are prevented from attending to noisier inputs like robot state and predicted stage. This is a clean, generalizable idea for V2D.
- **Learnable mixed-layer attention** (action expert attends to learned linear combinations of all VLM layers, init to identity); **delta actions + per-timestamp normalization**; **action compression via cubic spline** 26→20 steps for 1.3× speedup (cubic, not linear, to avoid jitter); 50 trainable task embeddings instead of language.
- Failure analysis: ~33% of failures are dexterity/clumsiness in grasp-release.

**2nd place, "Openpi Comet," https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10071** — q-score 0.2514 in-competition (11.4% success), 0.345 post-challenge. Two findings you should care about:
- **Temporal Ensemble and "Receding Temporal" control both scored 0% success.** Only **Receding Horizon** (execute the full action segment, then replan) worked. Their reading: long-horizon manipulation needs continuous feedback, and open-loop prediction averaging destroys it. **This is a direct counterexample to "just add temporal smoothing."** Action horizon 32 was optimal (non-monotonic); 30 Hz beat 15 Hz; absolute joint positions beat deltas.
- **Resolution mattered more than modality**: 224×224 → 720×480 (head) + 480×480 (wrist) **more than doubled success rate**, while RGB+Depth and RGB+PointCloud were only marginal over RGB-only.
- **Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning (RFT)** with pose perturbations: perturb initial robot poses, roll out, keep only successful episodes as new training data, 3 rounds × ~8.5K trajectories. Plus ~3.6K extra trajectories from motion planners and offline-RL rollouts on top of the 10K official teleop demos.

## G. RoboTwin Dual-Arm Collaboration Challenge (CVPR 2025 MEIS Workshop)
Report: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23351 · site https://robotwin-benchmark.github.io/cvpr-2025-challenge/ · platform https://robotwin-platform.github.io/
64 teams / 400+ participants; 3 stages; Round 2 added domain randomization (unseen backgrounds, ±3 cm table height, lighting, clutter); real round on AgileX COBOT-Magic with 300 medium-quality demos + 20 high-quality demos released one week before the deadline, evaluated 15 trials seen / 5 trials unseen background. **Best real-world team got 26.4/100** — the sim→real cliff is the headline.

- **AnchorDP3 (JD-TFS), simulation champion, 98.7%**: predicts **10–30 sparse geometrically meaningful keyposes** (pre-grasp, grasp, placement affordances) instead of dense 25 Hz action sequences — 14.5× more diverse data exposure per compute budget; **automatic point-level segmentation masks for task-critical objects** using privileged sim scene knowledge; **per-task lightweight encoders (0.28 M params each) feeding a shared diffusion expert** to prevent negative task interference; simultaneous joint-angle + EE-pose prediction with supervision on *all* predicted keyposes despite executing only the first.
- **SEM (TSAIL-HRL)**: **Spatial Enhancer** lifts 2D features to 3D by sampling candidate depths across views and projecting pixel features into 3D positional embeddings (keeps 2D semantics, adds metric spatial reasoning); robot-state encoder treats each joint as a graph node with attention over inter-joint distances; depth-aware encoding explicitly to resolve depth ambiguity in clutter. Their stated diagnosis: pure 2D encoders can't resolve depth ambiguity; EE-only state representations throw away proprioception.
- **MOMODA**: two-stage depth integration (align a frozen depth encoder to visual embeddings, then joint optimize); **temporal downsampling 25 Hz → 12 Hz** improved long-horizon prediction; scaling π₀ + 100→3000 episodes moved dual-shoes from 48.2% → 95.1%.
- **SHU-Robot**: the data-hygiene team — hybrid **automatic + manual trajectory filtering**, start-frame detection, motion segmentation, temporal trimming, length standardization, explicitly to remove inconsistent initial states, repeated grasps, arm jitter, irrelevant motions.
- **VMV**: simple high-level instructions generalized better than elaborate prompts; augment with instruction variants to absorb noisy/ambiguous language.

## H. MyoChallenge (NeurIPS 2022–2025) — noisy proprioception + heavy randomization
**2022 report: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v220/caggiano23a.html** (PDF: .../caggiano23a/caggiano23a.pdf). MyoHand: 29 bones, 23 joints, 39 muscles; 39-dim action in [0,1]. Randomization table (Phase 1 → Phase 2): die reorientation position ±10→±20 mm, orientation ±1.57→±3.14 rad, size fixed→±7 mm, mass 108 g→50–250 g, friction ±(1.0, 0.005, 0.0001)→±(0.2, 0.001, 0.00002); Baoding similar. 40 teams, 340+ submissions.
- **Die, PKU-MARL (1st ex-aequo)**: Natural Policy Gradient + reward shaping (touch / rotate / move-to-target terms) + **curriculum over both DR magnitude and goal range** + **multi-target training** (refresh the target on success so the agent must track a moving goal). 71% phase 1; in phase 2 they *deliberately gave up on large rotations* and optimized for ≤90°, landing 11%. Code: https://github.com/PKU-MARL/MyoChallenge
- **Die, IARAI-JKU (1st ex-aequo)**: three mechanisms to reduce task-irrelevant exploration — **performance-adaptive curriculum on the start-state distribution**, **potential-based shaping rewards**, **early termination on failure**. PGPE + ClipUp on evotorch, 3-layer RNN (22 k params), 256-CPU Ray cluster, population of 256 envs each adapting its own difficulty from the population's past performance, 12 B samples / 4 days. Best success 13% at the lowest effort 0.03. Code: https://github.com/iarai/MyoChallgenge-IARAI-JKU
- **Baoding, "stiff fingers" (1st)**: **Static-to-Dynamic Stabilization (SDS)** curriculum — first learn to hold the balls *still* at several intermediate points along the target trajectory from random phases, then gradually relax to moving targets and ramp target speed, then switch back to the canonical initial state once inertia exploitation becomes useful. PPO with **LSTM in both actor and critic** to handle partial observability. **100% phase 1, 55% phase 2**; ablations show training directly on the final task, "speed only," or "location only" all fail. Code: https://github.com/amathislab/myochallenge
- **Baoding, AL4Muscles (2nd)**: MPO (TonicRL) + **DEP-RL** for exploration through bottleneck states. Phase 2: abandoned the original task and trained a **surrogate** — static goals randomly initialized along the rotation ellipse, **randomized termination where success only counts after a randomly sampled, unobserved time interval** (so the policy must *stabilize*, not just touch), balls initialized from states recorded in previous episodes. Also **reconstructed missing joint velocities by finite-differencing successive observations** — the state space omitted them. 98% / 41%. Code: https://github.com/martius-lab/AL4MyoChallenge
- Organizers' stated lesson: "**Most winning solutions used curriculum learning. Some solutions used gradual domain randomization or an ensemble of networks for task specialization.**" AL4Muscles: "reward design, state initialization and termination conditions had the strongest effect"; a *bigger* state space often made learning infeasible while the original induced partial observability.

**Lattice** (grew out of the 2022 Baoding win, used for MyoChallenge 2023): https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20065 — inject **temporally-correlated noise into the policy's latent state** rather than into actions, so exploration noise respects learned actuator synergies: a ~ 𝒩(Wx, WΣ_x Wᵀ + Σ_a). 20–60% energy reduction on MyoSuite reach/manipulation.

**MyoChallenge 2025** (NeurIPS 2025, 4th edition): https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15650 · https://www.myosuite.org/myochallenge/myochallenge-2025 · env repo https://github.com/MyoHub/myochallenge_2025eval. ~70 teams, 560+ submissions; final scores = average over **100 unseen trials**.
- Table tennis 1st **ActingAI ("Diff-Muscle")** 100% / 64% phase 2, effort 0.1306: physics-based ball-trajectory planner producing racket position/velocity/orientation targets → PPO policy in *joint* space → **Kinematics-based Muscle Actuation Controller**. Core claim: musculoskeletal systems are conditionally **differentially flat** w.r.t. joint configuration, so you can compress the action space from ℝ²⁷³ muscle activations to ℝ³² joints without losing dynamical information.
- 2nd **BioSyn**: **synergy subnetworks** per body region (hand / arm / torso / pelvis) with the synergy weights folded into exploration via multivariate Gaussian noise; **weakness-aware curriculum** that up-weights underperforming stroke patterns late in training.
- 3rd **LNSGroup**: CrossQ (no target networks, batchnorm-friendly) + **specialized forehand/backhand sub-policies routed at runtime by a Gaussian Process Classifier** that estimates each policy's success probability from incoming-ball features. Divide-and-conquer routing under stochastic inputs — same idea as HCB-Hand's MoE.
- Soccer 1st **Servette MyoClub** (only team above baseline; 100% / 32%): the most V2D-shaped pipeline in the whole survey — **teacher = pre-trained KINESIS locomotion prior** (motion imitation on 1.8 h of KIT-Locomotion, MoE of 3 deep-MLP experts + gating, 290 muscles), fine-tuned target-reaching → kicking, then **retargeted to the challenge observation space via offline trajectory collection with success filtering → student pre-training → on-policy behavior cloning → RL fine-tuning**, with **base-policy rollouts under domain randomization**. Organizers' summary: "extracting successful trajectories and refining them via on-policy behavior cloning **under environmental noise** mimics the repetitive drilling required to establish robust motor control."
- Cross-track lesson: all three table-tennis medalists **abandoned end-to-end learning for explicit physics models + task priors**.

## I. Amazon Picking / Robotics Challenge — the classic perception lessons
**Correll et al., "Analysis and Observations from the First APC," https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.05484.** 26-team survey. Perception was ranked the hardest problem (**4.52/5**). 20 teams used structured-light 3D; 75% PCL, 67% OpenCV. Killers: the rubber duck shipping sometimes in a plastic bag and sometimes not ("dramatic impact on perception"), reflective/transparent surfaces, and the black wire-mesh pencil cup that "foiled most sensors." Notably **no clear correlation between a team's perception background and their ranking**. What the top teams actually did: **RBO (1st) combined multiple complementary features (color, edge, height) for detection and filtering**; several teams mounted cameras at multiple locations to cut occlusion; **Team K used manipulation as perception** — pick the object up, identify it, put it back if wrong.

**MIT-Princeton ARC 2017 (1st in stowing), https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.01330.** Grasp-first-then-recognize. Noise handling, concretely:
- **Hole-filling on missing depth + triangulation across camera views**; objects with no depth get a synthetic 3 cm height inserted before the FCN.
- **Two RGB-D views registered and orthographically projected into heightmaps** — redundancy against single-camera failure.
- **Background subtraction** using empty-bin reference images.
- **Four complementary grasping primitives** (suction down/side, grasp down, flush grasp) with a task planner that re-weights them online: suction prioritized γ=1.0 for the first 3 min, **failed attempts block nearby proposals within 2 cm**, and γ drops to 0.5 after 2 failures, 0.25 after 3+. Suction 58.3%, parallel-jaw 75%.
- **Cross-domain image matching** for novel objects: freeze ImageNet weights on the product-image stream, train only the observed-image stream (prevents metric collapse); **multi-anchor switching** picks which product image to compare against by L2 feature distance. 93.6% known / 77.5% novel.
- Trained on only **1,837 hand-labeled RGB-D images** with ±1.6 cm jitter augmentation on a ResNet-101 backbone.
- Honest postmortem: a perception node lost its RGB-D connection mid-competition with no reconnection logic → hard reset; state tracking was "particularly sensitive to drastic changes" when multiple objects moved at once, with no recovery. **Average pick success across all ARC teams was 62%** vs published lab numbers.
Related: Team Delft (APC 2016 winner) https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05514; MIT-Princeton APC 2016 multi-view self-supervised 6D pose https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09475.

## J. Driving / control competitions with explicit noise-in-the-loop
**AI Driving Olympics (NeurIPS 2018/2019), https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.02503.** Duckietown, same Docker container in sim and on the real Duckiebot. Sim-to-real gaps: venue lighting vs training lighting, camera calibration differences breaking color-based lane detection, per-robot motor gain variation. **Most finalists lasted ~3 tiles before leaving the lane.** Winner (Wei Gao) used a modular pipeline — semantic segmentation → ego-centric 3D reconstruction + lane curvature → adaptive pure pursuit — beating the RL (DDPG/TD3) and IL entries. Organizers' conclusion: "methods developed in simulation may have considerable difficulty in reaching the performance of methods developed on physical hardware." They also flag their own evaluation design failure: distance-based scoring was gamed by spinning in circles, and single-map single-episode evaluation lacked statistical power.

**AI Olympics with RealAIGym (IJCAI'23, IROS'24, ICRA'25, IJCAI-ECAI'26)** — the only competition I found with a *formal explicit robustness score*. Lessons paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15290. The robustness score S_r averages 6 criteria: **model inaccuracies** (vary one sim parameter at a time while the controller keeps the nominal model), **velocity measurement noise**, **torque noise**, **delayed torque response** (τ = τ_{t−1} + k_resp(τ_des − τ_{t−1})), **perturbations**, **constant torque disturbance**. Winning/finalist methods:
- **EvolSAC** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10030): SAC on a physics-inspired surrogate reward, then **fine-tune with Separable Natural Evolution Strategy directly against the competition score function** (which includes the robustness terms). Gradient-free finetuning of a learned policy against a rugged/noisy objective — preserve only top-K.
- **HistorySAC** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20096): you can't infer link masses from a single state, so **encode the last 11 velocity measurements + current through two 1-D convs (kernel 5, 12 channels) + two FC layers into a learned context** concatenated to the current state. Plus: **use only negative rewards (punishments) capped at 0** — makes the Q-function far more stable and prevents policy degradation the moment a successful swing-up appears. Plus **system ID by Differential Evolution** with an importance-weighted squared error that linearly decays from 1 → 0.5 over the trajectory (so accumulated late-trajectory error counts less) on only the first 1.5 s; then **train across a multi-environment set of several SysID solutions** to avoid overfitting one identified model.
- **AR-EAPO** (average-reward MaxEnt RL): entropy term prevents the pendulum from parking at the top, producing endless swing-up/swing-down diversity that yields a robust policy.
- Also: MC-PILCO model-based RL (https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06721), real-time nonlinear MPC (https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05363, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09272).

**DARPA SubT — CERBERUS, https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07067.** Degradation modes named explicitly: complete darkness, low texture, geometric self-similarity, thermal flatness, obscurants (smoke, fog, dust). Fixes: **Visual-Thermal-Inertial Odometry that balances the share of tracked features from each image stream by spatial and temporal entropy of image quality** — i.e. adaptive per-sensor weighting under degradation, not fixed fusion; LED flashing synchronized to camera exposure on aerial robots to beat dust and darkness; three terrain sensors benchmarked (RealSense / Pico Monstar / Robosense BPearl) with BPearl selected for robustness; state machine safety layers that **reverse and replan when traversability estimates are corrupted** rather than pushing through. They also report that model-based locomotion controllers failed on "corrupted local terrain representations" in the Tunnel Circuit while **RL-based locomotion was robust** in the Urban Circuit. (Team CoSTAR/NeBula is the complementary read: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.11470.)

## K. ManiSkill family
- ManiSkill 2021 challenge submission (no-interaction track): https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14646 — multi-object point-cloud features into a deep+wide transformer predicting action scores; the paper is explicitly "a bag of tricks and abortive attempts."
- **ManiSkill-ViTac 2025**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12503 · https://ai-workshops.github.io/maniskill-vitac-challenge-2025/. Three tracks: (1) tactile-only manipulation (peg insertion, lock opening, shape generalization), score = 50×SR₁ + 50×SR₂; (2) tactile+vision fusion with RealSense D415 depth + semantic segmentation, score = 100×SR; (3) **tactile sensor structure design** (GelSight Mini silicone shape + marker distribution), 50×SR + 50×subjective. ## Cross-cutting pattern table (what actually wins when perception is noisy)

| Family | Representative winners | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Validity-gated temporal filtering** (not blind smoothing) | RRC'22 superiordinosaur (delay/confidence gate → moving average, else hold last pose); ACE ARCTIC (carry previous vertices only if loss < threshold); BEHAVIOR 1st (majority vote over 3 stage predictions) | Blind interpolation/averaging compounds error across consecutive bad frames; a gate stops it |
| **Test-time optimization against physics** | JVhands "Neural Smooth" (optimize per-sequence predictions with 2D-projection + acceleration losses) | Exactly V2D Track 1's physical-plausibility axis; uses the modality that generalizes (2D) to fix the one that doesn't (3D) |
| **Train on the noise you'll see** | JVhands extrinsic-translation noise aug; RRC Allshire DR + keypoints; CHORD noise ablation (10–60 mm wrist pos, 5–30° wrist ori, 5–30 mm contact pos, 5–30° contact normal, random-walk); RoboTwin Round 2 DR | Tier-2 of V2D Track 2 is literally this |
| **Mixture of experts / routing under noisy obs** | HCB-Hand MoRE (4 sparse + 1 shared, top-1); DexGrasp'26 1st (residual MoE); LNSGroup GPC routing; AnchorDP3 per-task encoders + shared diffusion expert | Noise makes the conditional action distribution multimodal; one net regresses to the mean |
| **Privileged-teacher → student distillation** | Servette MyoClub (KINESIS teacher → offline rollouts with success filtering + DR → on-policy BC → RL finetune); ManipTrans/HCB two-stage; DexMachina's own stated plan | Standard answer to "clean reference, noisy deployment" |
| **Expert-data filtering** | RRC'22 excludedrice (iterative semi-supervised expert classifier); RoboTwin SHU-Robot (hybrid auto+manual trajectory filtering); BEHAVIOR 2nd RFT (keep only successful perturbed rollouts); Servette (success filtering) | BC on mixed-quality data is worse than BC on a filtered expert subset |
| **Representation choices that absorb noise** | keypoints not quaternions (Allshire, DexMachina, CHORD); depth discretized to one-hot bins (Partsey, +50% noise tolerance); translation-invariant vs -variant feature split (JVhands); contact-wrench instead of contact-location (CHORD) | Push the noise into a subspace the policy doesn't depend on |
| **Auxiliary/self-supervised objectives** | Ye et al. 6 aux tasks (CPC\|A, PBL, ADP, GID, CP); Partsey geometric-invariance losses | Constrains recurrent dynamics to low dimension → generalizes under noisy segmentation |
| **Cheap ensembling** | Partsey dropout-as-ensemble (118.8 vs 8.45 FPS); BEHAVIOR 1st multi-sample flow matching (15 samples/VLM pass) | Ensemble benefit without ensemble cost |
| **Explicit failure-detection + retry rules** | BEHAVIOR 1st gripper reopen rule (≈2× success on 13 tasks); UniTeam rotate-and-retry + pick verification; MIT-Princeton primitive re-weighting after failures | Cheapest wins per line of code, repeatedly |
| **Structured priors > end-to-end** | All three MyoChallenge'25 table-tennis medalists; AI-DO winner; RRC'20 state machines; CERBERUS state machine safety layers | Under noise, a physics model gives you a target the policy only has to track |

**One important negative result**: Openpi Comet found **Temporal Ensemble and Receding Temporal control scored 0%** on BEHAVIOR; only receding-horizon (execute full segment, replan) worked. Don't assume action-level temporal smoothing is free — it can be catastrophic on long-horizon tasks.
