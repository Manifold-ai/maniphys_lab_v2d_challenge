# Learning from Imperfect / Suboptimal / Noisy Demonstrations — RL & IL Literature

**Legend for the portability note:** the target setting is *online RL in sim, policy tracks a noisy reference trajectory, dense per-timestep tracking reward, ground-truth sim state available*. That setting differs from most of this literature in three ways: (a) you have an environment and can roll out, (b) you already have a dense reward signal, (c) the "demonstration" is a **reference trajectory (state-only, per-timestep)**, not state-action pairs, and the noise is in the *target*, not in the *actions*. What transfers is the **weighting / confidence / filtering machinery**, not the reward-learning machinery.

## 1. The T-REX / REX family (ranking-based reward extrapolation)

### T-REX — Extrapolating Beyond Suboptimal Demonstrations via IRL from Observations
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06387
- Code: https://github.com/hiwonjoon/ICML2019-TREX (MIT, atari/ + mujoco/)
- ICML 2019. Brown, Goo, Nagarajan, Niekum.
- **Mechanism:** Learns a state-level reward network $\hat r_\theta(s)$ from *ranked* trajectories using a Bradley–Terry / Luce–Shepard pairwise loss over **sums** of predicted rewards: $\mathcal{L}(\theta)=-\sum_{\tau_i\prec\tau_j}\log\frac{\exp\sum_{s\in\tau_j}\hat r_\theta(s)}{\exp\sum_{s\in\tau_i}\hat r_\theta(s)+\exp\sum_{s\in\tau_j}\hat r_\theta(s)}$. Crucially it trains on **random length-$L$ sub-snippets** (L=50 for MuJoCo; 50–100 with temporal alignment $t_i\le t_j$ for Atari; ~5–6k pairs) rather than whole trajectories, which is both data augmentation and an implicit "later ⇒ better" prior. The learned reward is then optimized with PPO (MuJoCo: ensemble of 5 reward nets, averaged + control penalty; Atari: sigmoid-squashed reward, 50M frames). The point is that a reward fit to *rank order* can be extrapolated past the best demo, unlike max-entropy IRL which makes the demonstrator look optimal.
- **Portability:** Medium-low as-is (you don't need a learned reward), but the **snippet-level pairwise ranking loss is directly reusable as a per-segment quality scorer** over reference-trajectory windows — e.g. rank motion segments by tracking feasibility and use the score as a reward weight.

### D-REX — Better-than-Demonstrator IL via Automatically-Ranked Demonstrations
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03976
- Code: https://github.com/dsbrown1331/CoRL2019-DREX — project page https://dsbrown1331.github.io/CoRL2019-DREX/
- CoRL 2019. Brown, Goo, Niekum.
- **Mechanism:** Removes T-REX's need for human rankings. (1) BC on the unranked demos → $\pi_{BC}$. (2) **Synthesize a ranking by noise injection**: roll out $\epsilon$-greedy $\pi_{BC}$ at K rollouts × several noise levels (MuJoCo: 20 levels on $[0,1)$; Atari: $\mathcal{E}=(1.0,0.75,0.5,0.25,0.02)$, K=20), and declare $\tau_i \prec \tau_j$ iff $\epsilon_i > \epsilon_j$. (3) Run the T-REX pairwise loss on these auto-ranked rollouts. (4) RL on $\hat R$. Theorem 1 gives a sufficient condition for better-than-demonstrator performance: $J(\pi^*_{R^*}|R^*) - J(\mathcal{D}|R^*) > \epsilon_\Phi + \frac{2\|\epsilon\|_\infty}{1-\gamma}$, i.e. extrapolation is guaranteed when the demonstrator is sufficiently suboptimal *and* reward-approximation error is small.
- **Portability:** **High conceptually.** The "inject graded noise into a base policy to manufacture a quality ordering for free" trick maps cleanly onto your setting: inject graded noise into the *reference trajectory* and learn a monotone quality/confidence head. That gives you a self-supervised noise-level estimator without labels.

### SSRR — Learning from Suboptimal Demonstration via Self-Supervised Reward Regression
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11723
- Code: https://github.com/CORE-Robotics-Lab/SSRR
- CoRL 2020 (PMLR v155, published 2021). Chen, Paleja, Gombolay.
- **Mechanism:** Argues D-REX's Luce–Shepard ranking loss is *wrong*, because ranking is purely ordinal: for three noise levels $\eta_1<\eta_2<\eta_3$ the pairwise loss forces $r_2=(r_1+r_3)/2$ regardless of whether $\eta_2$ is 1% or 50% — the *interval* information in $\eta$ is discarded. SSRR instead (1) generates $\tau_\eta \sim \pi_\eta(a|s)=\eta\,\mathcal{U}(a)+(1-\eta)\pi_{BC}(a|s)$, (2) empirically **fits a sigmoid noise–performance curve** $\sigma(\eta)=\frac{c}{1+\exp(-k(\eta-x_0))}+y_0$, and (3) regresses the reward directly onto that curve: $\mathcal{L}_{SSRR}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau_i}\big[\big(\sum_t R_\theta(s^i_t,a^i_t)-\sigma(\eta_i)\big)^2\big]$, then SAC on $R_\theta$. Reported ~0.95 correlation with ground-truth reward vs ~0.75 for T-REX/D-REX; ~200% over the demonstrator. Real robot demo: table-tennis topspin strike.
- **Portability:** **High.** The regression-to-a-fitted-degradation-curve idea is exactly what you want if you can parameterize reference-noise magnitude: fit "tracking performance vs. injected reference noise" once, then use it to calibrate a per-trajectory (or per-segment) confidence, rather than a bare ordinal ranking.

### S3RR — Towards Sample-efficient Apprenticeship Learning from Suboptimal Demonstration
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04347
- No standalone code found (SSRR repo is the reference implementation)
- AI-HRI symposium @ AAAI-FSS 2021. Chen, Paleja, Gombolay.
- **Mechanism:** Direct follow-up to SSRR. Identifies that SSRR's *random* noise injection is inconsistent — results depend on which state variables get perturbed, and random degradation doesn't span the realistic space of suboptimality. Replaces it with **systematic** trajectory-degradation strategies to synthesize the optimality-parameterized dataset, improving reward/ground-truth correlation at lower sample cost.
- **Portability:** High and cheap — it's a statement that *how* you corrupt matters more than *how much*. In your setting: corrupt the reference in structured, physically meaningful ways (joint-angle jitter vs. root-drift vs. foot-skate vs. timing warp) rather than isotropic Gaussian noise.

### Bayesian REX — Safe Imitation Learning via Fast Bayesian Reward Inference from Preferences
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09089 (earlier workshop version: https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04472)
- Code: https://github.com/dsbrown1331/bayesianrex
- ICML 2020. Brown, Goo, Niekum.
- **Mechanism:** Makes T-REX Bayesian. Pre-trains a low-dim state feature encoder $\phi(s)$ via self-supervised auxiliary tasks, then assumes the reward is **linear in those frozen features**, $R(s)=w^\top\phi(s)$. Because the return of a fixed trajectory is then linear in $w$, the Bradley–Terry preference likelihood is cheap, and MCMC over $w$ yields 100k posterior samples in ~5 min on a laptop. The posterior enables high-confidence performance bounds / risk-aware policy evaluation (value-at-risk over reward posterior) — its selling point is *safety*, not raw return.
- **Portability:** Medium. The "freeze a feature encoder, make the scoring head linear, then you get a cheap posterior" trick is a reasonable way to get **uncertainty estimates over per-frame reference quality**, which you could use to down-weight tracking terms where the reference is unreliable.

## 2. Confidence-weighted / imperfect-demonstration adversarial IL

### 2IWIL and IC-GAIL — Imitation Learning from Imperfect Demonstration
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09387
- Code: https://github.com/kristery/Imitation-Learning-from-Imperfect-Demonstration
- ICML 2019. Wu, Charoenphakdee, Bao, Tangkaratt, Sugiyama.
- **Mechanism:** Both assume a *small* subset of demos carries confidence labels $r(s,a)=p(y=+1|s,a)$ and the rest are unlabeled. **2IWIL** is two-step: first a semi-supervised confidence classifier minimizing $R_{SC,\ell}(g)=\mathbb{E}_{x,r\sim q}[r(\ell(g(x))-\ell(-g(x)))+(1-\beta)\ell(-g(x))]+\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p}[\beta\ell(-g(x))]$ (the $\beta$ trades variance between labeled and unlabeled parts); then plug the predicted confidences in as importance weights in a reweighted GAIL objective $\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_\theta}[\log D_w]+\mathbb{E}_{x,r\sim q}[\tfrac{r}{\alpha}\log(1-D_w)]$, $\alpha$ = class prior. **IC-GAIL** avoids the two-stage estimation error by instead matching against the *non-optimal* mixture $p'=\alpha p_\theta+(1-\alpha)p_{non}$: $\min_\theta\max_w \mathbb{E}_{x\sim p}[\log(1-D_w)]+\lambda\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_\theta}[\log D_w]+(1-\lambda)\mathbb{E}_{x,r\sim q}[\tfrac{1-r}{1-\alpha}\log D_w]$ with $\lambda=\max\{\tau,\hat\alpha\}$ for stability when optimal demos are scarce.
- **Portability:** Medium. Adversarial occupancy matching is the wrong frame for you (you have a dense reward), but **the importance-weight form $w \propto r/\alpha$ and the semi-supervised confidence classifier (label a small subset of reference clips by hand, propagate to the rest) is directly usable** as a per-clip reward scale.

### WGAIL — Learning to Weight Imperfect Demonstrations
- **No arXiv version.** PMLR: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/wang21aa.html
- Code: https://github.com/yunke-wang/WGAIL (also contains 2IWIL, GAIL, T-REX baselines; Atari code split out)
- ICML 2021. Wang, Xu, Du, Lee.
- **Mechanism:** Removes 2IWIL's dependence on oracle confidence labels entirely. Derives demonstration weights **from quantities already inside GAIL** — the discriminator output and the current policy — rather than from external annotation, so the weights co-evolve with training. Demos the discriminator finds "expert-like relative to the current policy" get upweighted; the rest are suppressed, letting the agent exceed the imperfect expert on MuJoCo and Atari.
- **Portability:** High-concept, low-code. The lesson — **derive the weight from a quantity you already compute, don't add an annotation pipeline** — is the right instinct for your setting: derive a per-frame reference confidence from the tracking-error residual itself, or from a critic's value estimate.

### SAIL — Robust Adversarial Imitation Learning via Adaptively-Selected Demonstrations
- Code: https://github.com/yunke-wang/SAIL — IJCAI 2021, Wang et al. No arXiv found.
- **Mechanism:** Adaptively *selects* (rather than weights) the subset of demonstrations used by the adversarial objective at each iteration, based on their agreement with the current policy. Predecessor of the same group's UID/PU line.
- **Portability:** Medium — selection thresholds are a cruder but more stable alternative to soft weights when the noise is heavy-tailed.

### VILD — Variational Imitation Learning with Diverse-quality Demonstrations
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06769
- PMLR: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/tangkaratt20a.html — **no official GitHub found** (author's related code lives at https://github.com/voot-t/, but VILD is not there under that name)
- ICML 2020. Tangkaratt, Han, Khan, Sugiyama.
- **Mechanism:** Puts demonstrator expertise into an explicit **probabilistic graphical model**: each demonstrator $k$ has a latent noise/expertise parameter, and observed actions are noisy corruptions of the (unknown) optimal action. Jointly estimates the reward and the per-demonstrator expertise via **variational inference** with an importance-sampling correction. The paper's key technical point is that the naive expertise estimator degenerates in high-dimensional continuous control, and the variational approximation (reducible to standard RL updates) fixes it.
- **Portability:** **High for your setting.** VILD is the cleanest statement of "model the corruption process explicitly and infer its parameters." If your reference trajectories come from N different sources (mocap vs. video-retargeted vs. generated), a VILD-style per-source latent noise scale, estimated online, is a direct fit.

### CAIL — Confidence-Aware Imitation Learning from Demonstrations with Varying Optimality
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14754
- Code: https://github.com/Stanford-ILIAD/Confidence-Aware-Imitation-Learning
- NeurIPS 2021. Zhang, Cao, Sadigh, Sui.
- **Mechanism:** A genuine **bilevel** formulation. *Inner loss*: confidence-reweighted AIRL (discriminator + generator terms weighted by per-sample $\beta(s,a)$), giving $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\mu\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_{in}(s,a;\theta_t,\beta_{\tau+1})$. *Outer loss*: a **ranking loss on a small ranked evaluation set**, $\mathcal{L}_{out}(\theta_D)=\sum_i\sum_{j>i}\mathrm{RK}[\eta'_{\xi_i},\eta'_{\xi_j};\mathbb{1}[\eta_{\xi_i}>\eta_{\xi_j}]]$ — i.e. does the learned reward rank held-out trajectories correctly? Confidence is then updated by differentiating the outer loss through a *pseudo-updated* policy: $\beta_{\tau+1}=\beta_\tau-\alpha\nabla_\beta\mathcal{L}_{out}(\theta')$. Comes with convergence guarantees and works even with **zero optimal demonstrations** in the set.
- **Portability:** **High.** This is the single most structurally relevant method: "learn a per-sample weight by backpropagating a *held-out evaluation* signal through the policy update." In your setting the outer signal is free — it's your actual tracking success / task return in sim — so you can drop CAIL's ranking-loss proxy and use real rollout return as the outer objective. This is a meta-gradient reward-weighting scheme.

### UID — Unlabeled Imperfect Demonstrations in Adversarial Imitation Learning
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06271
- Code: https://github.com/yunke-wang/UID
- AAAI 2023. Wang, Du, Xu.
- **Mechanism:** Treats the demo set as **positive-unlabeled** rather than all-positive: only an unknown fraction is optimal. Builds a PU-learning-corrected discriminator and adds self-paced sampling — it dynamically samples the demonstrations that best match the *current* agent policy's trajectory distribution, so the effective expert set ratchets upward in quality as the agent improves.
- **Portability:** Medium-high — the PU correction is the right statistical framing whenever you have "mostly-good reference data with an unknown fraction of garbage."

### CIQL — Aligning Human Intent from Imperfect Demonstrations with Confidence-based Inverse soft-Q Learning
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11194
- Code: https://github.com/XizoB/CIQL
- IEEE RA-L 9(8):7150–7157, 2024. Bu, Li, Liu, Ma, Huang.
- **Mechanism:** Gets **transition-level** (not trajectory-level) confidence scores without ground-truth reward or active human queries, and feeds them into IQ-Learn (inverse soft-Q). Reports ~40% average success-rate improvement over the baseline. Its most useful empirical finding: **penalization beats filtering** — softly downweighting bad transitions aligns better with human intent than hard-removing them.
- **Portability:** **High, and the finding matters for you.** Per-timestep confidence at the transition level is exactly the granularity of a per-frame tracking reward, and "soft down-weight, don't hard-mask" is directly actionable in a dense tracking reward.

## 3. The DICE family (offline distribution-correction estimation)

### OptiDICE — Offline Policy Optimization via Stationary Distribution Correction Estimation
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10783
- Code: https://github.com/secury/optidice
- ICML 2021. Lee, Jeon, Lee, Pineau, Kim.
- **Mechanism:** The foundation of the family. Estimates the **stationary distribution correction ratio** $w(s,a)=d^{\pi^*}(s,a)/d^{D}(s,a)$ of the optimal policy directly, with no policy gradient and no bootstrapped Q. Derives a closed-form solution for the inner maximization so the whole thing collapses to an **unconstrained convex problem**, which is why the family is stable where minimax adversarial IL is not.
- **Portability:** Low — it's a pure offline-RL method and your setting is online with a reward. Listed because it's the mathematical ancestor of the three below.

### DemoDICE — Offline Imitation Learning with Supplementary Imperfect Demonstrations
- **No arXiv version.** OpenReview: https://openreview.net/forum?id=BrPdX1bDZkQ (PDF: https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BrPdX1bDZkQ)
- Code: https://github.com/KAIST-AILab/imitation-dice (mirror: https://github.com/geon-hyeong/imitation-dice)
- ICLR 2022. Kim, Seo, Lee, Jeon, Hwang, Yang, Kim.
- **Mechanism:** Two datasets — a small expert set $\mathcal{D}_E$ and a large imperfect/mixed set $\mathcal{D}_O$. Objective is $\min_\pi D_{KL}(d^\pi\|d^E)+\alpha D_{KL}(d^\pi\|d^O)$: match the expert's state-action occupancy while a KL regularizer against the *full* dataset keeps the policy inside the supported region (this is what stops offline IL from collapsing on the narrow expert support). Via Lagrangian/Fenchel duality this reduces from a minimax adversarial problem to **direct convex optimization**, which is the paper's headline stability claim. A discriminator supplies $\log(d^E/d^O)$; policy extraction is weighted BC.
- **Portability:** Low-medium. The **structural idea — "anchor to the clean subset, regularize toward the full noisy set with weight $\alpha$" — is portable** as a two-term reward: track the trusted reference, plus a weaker regularizer toward the full (noisy) reference set. The dual machinery itself is not needed online.

### SMODICE — Versatile Offline Imitation from Observations and Examples via Regularized State-Occupancy Matching
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02433
- Code: https://github.com/JasonMa2016/SMODICE
- ICML 2022. Ma, Shen, Jayaraman, Bastani.
- **Mechanism:** **State-occupancy** matching (no expert actions needed): $\min_\pi D_{KL}(d^\pi(s)\|d^E(s))$, upper-bounded as $\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^\pi}[\log\frac{d^O(s)}{d^E(s)}]+D_f(d^\pi(s,a)\|d^O(s,a))$ so it can be optimized offline. A state-only discriminator gives $R(s)=-\log(1/c^*(s)-1)$; Fenchel duality turns the constrained occupancy problem into $\min_{V\ge0}(1-\gamma)\mathbb{E}_{\mu_0}[V]+\mathbb{E}_{d^O}[f_*(R(s)+\gamma \mathcal{T}V(s,a)-V(s))]$; importance weights fall out as $\xi^*=f'_*(R+\gamma\mathcal{T}V^*-V^*)$; policy via **weighted BC** $\min_\pi -\mathbb{E}_{d^O}[\xi^*(s,a)\log\pi(a|s)]$. Handles dynamics/morphology-mismatched experts and example-based RL in the same framework.
- **Portability:** **Medium-high — this is the most structurally analogous of the DICE family**, because it is *state-only*, which is what a reference trajectory is. The morphology-mismatch setting (expert has different embodiment) is the same problem class as "human mocap retargeted to a humanoid with different kinematics." The final weighted-BC form $\xi^*(s,a)\log\pi(a|s)$ is a drop-in template for a state-derived per-sample weight.

### LobsDICE — Offline Learning from Observation via Stationary Distribution Correction Estimation
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13536
- Code: https://github.com/KAIST-AILab/imitation-dice
- NeurIPS 2022. Kim, Lee, Jang, Yang, Kim.
- **Mechanism:** LfO variant: state-only expert demos, plus action-labeled transitions from agents of *unknown quality*. Minimizes the divergence between expert and agent **state-transition** distributions $d(s,s')$ (not just $d(s)$) as a **single convex minimization** — the transition-level matching is the delta vs. SMODICE and is what disambiguates policies that visit the same states in the wrong order.
- **Portability:** Medium-high for the same reason as SMODICE, with a bonus: **matching $d(s,s')$ rather than $d(s)$ is the right object for motion tracking**, where velocity/transition structure is the thing you actually care about and where noisy references break down first.

### DILO (related, useful) — A Dual Approach to Imitation Learning from Observations with Offline Datasets
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08805 — Sikchi, Chuck, Zhang, Niekum. Dual framework that improves on SMODICE's upper-bound looseness.

## 4. DWBC — Discriminator-Weighted Offline Imitation Learning from Suboptimal Demonstrations

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10050
- Code: https://github.com/ryanxhr/DWBC
- ICML 2022. Xu, Zhan, Yin, Qin.
- **Mechanism:** Trains a discriminator $d$ and a BC policy **cooperatively rather than adversarially**, in one stage, with no separate reward-learning or offline-RL phase. The discriminator is trained with a **PU-learning-corrected** objective: $\eta\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_e}[-\log d]+\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_o}[-\log(1-d)]-\eta\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_e}[-\log(1-d)]$, where $\eta$ is the positive-class prior (the third term is the PU correction that prevents the unlabeled set from being treated as pure negatives). The key trick: **the policy's own log-likelihood is an input to the discriminator**, $d(s,a,\log\pi(a|s))$, so the two networks inform each other. Final policy loss is weighted BC with $w=\alpha-\frac{\eta}{d(1-d)}$ on expert data and $w=\frac{1}{1-d}$ on the mixed data.
- **Portability:** **High.** DWBC is the cleanest "one-stage discriminator-weighted" recipe in the literature, and the $\log\pi(a|s)$-into-the-discriminator trick generalizes: in your setting, feed **the policy's current tracking error at that frame** into the confidence head, so the confidence estimate is conditioned on what the policy can actually achieve rather than on the reference alone. That closes the same loop DWBC closes.

## 5. ILEED — Imitation Learning by Estimating Expertise of Demonstrators

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01288
- Code: https://github.com/Stanford-ILIAD/ILEED
- ICML 2022. Beliaev, Shih, Ermon, Sadigh, Pedarsani.
- **Mechanism:** Models **state-dependent** expertise, not a scalar per demonstrator. Two embeddings: state embedding $f_\varphi:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}^d$ and demonstrator embedding $\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$, combined as $\rho_\varphi(s,\omega_i)=\sigma(\langle f_\varphi(s),\omega_i\rangle)$. Demonstrator $i$'s action distribution is an **interpolation between the optimal policy and noise**: discrete, $\pi(a|s,\omega_i)=\rho\,\pi^*_\theta(a|s)+(1-\rho)/|\mathcal{A}|$; continuous, a GMM with **variance inflated by $1/\rho$**: $\sum_j\alpha_j\mathcal{N}(a;\mu^*_j(s),\sigma^*_j(s)/\rho_\varphi(s,\omega_i))$. Everything is trained by joint MLE $\mathcal{L}(\theta,\varphi,\omega)=-\mathbb{E}_{i,(s,a)}[\log\pi(a|s,\omega_i,\varphi,\pi_\theta)]$ — completely unsupervised, no expertise labels. Wins 21/23 settings on Robomimic, MiniGrid, chess.
- **Portability:** **High, and this is the one I'd reach for first.** Two reasons: (a) the continuous-action parameterization *is* a per-state inverse-variance weight — $\rho_\varphi(s,\omega_i)$ literally scales the Gaussian precision, which is mathematically the same object as a per-frame weight on a squared tracking error; (b) it's **state-dependent**, so it models "this mocap clip is clean during locomotion but garbage during the hand-contact phase," which is exactly the failure mode of retargeted references. Replace "demonstrator index $i$" with "reference-clip index" and it ports almost verbatim.

## 6. RIL-Co — Robust Imitation Learning from Noisy Demonstrations

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10181
- Code: https://github.com/voot-t/ril_co
- AISTATS 2021 (PMLR 130:298–306). Tangkaratt, Charoenphakdee, Sugiyama.
- **Mechanism:** Two ingredients, and the paper shows **you need both**. (1) **Symmetric loss.** Recasts IL as binary classification with balanced risk $\mathcal{R}(g;\rho',\rho_\pi^\lambda,\ell_{sym})=\tfrac12\mathbb{E}_{\rho'}[\ell_{sym}(g)]+\tfrac12\mathbb{E}_{\rho_\pi^\lambda}[\ell_{sym}(-g)]$ where $\rho_\pi^\lambda=\lambda\rho_N+(1-\lambda)\rho_\pi$ mixes in the noise density, and solves $\max_\pi\min_g\mathcal{R}$. A loss is symmetric iff $\ell(z)+\ell(-z)=c$; this is the classical condition under which the risk minimizer is unchanged by symmetric label noise. They use the **AP (active–passive) loss** — normalized logistic + sigmoid — because the unhinged loss $\ell(z)=1-z$ is symmetric but unbounded below and underfits. (2) **Co-pseudo-labeling.** Two classifiers $g_1,g_2$ on disjoint splits; each is trained on its own split *plus* pseudo-labels from its peer (samples where the peer scores $<0$), which suppresses the self-confirmation bias that single-network pseudo-labeling suffers from. $\lambda=0.5$ is derived, not tuned. Reward for the RL step is $r(x)=\ell_{sym}(-g_1(x))$.
- **Portability:** **The symmetric-loss half is very portable; the co-labeling half less so.** Concretely: if your tracking reward is $\exp(-\|q-q^{ref}\|^2)$, that's an MSE-flavored, *asymmetric* objective — a single badly-retargeted frame dominates the gradient. Swapping the inner metric for a bounded/symmetric one (see §10) is a one-line change with real robustness gains. The co-training half needs a classification problem you don't naturally have.

Companion: **Robust Imitation Learning from Corrupted Demonstrations** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12594 (Liu, Tang, Li, Luo, 2022, no code found). Replaces the BC mean loss with a **Median-of-Means** objective: partition into groups, take the mean per group, then the median across groups. Proves nearly the same error scaling and sample complexity as clean-data BC even with a constant fraction of *adversarially* corrupted demos. **Portability: high and trivially cheap** — MoM over minibatch groups of the tracking loss is ~5 lines and gives adversarial-corruption robustness for free.

## 7. ADAIL and confidence-conditioned policies

### ADAIL — Adaptive Adversarial Imitation Learning
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12647 — Lu & Tompson, 2020. No code found.
- **Mechanism:** ⚠️ **This is not an imperfect-demonstration method.** ADAIL is about *dynamics* variation: learn a policy that transfers across environments with different physical parameters from demos collected in one source domain, using a learned **dynamics embedding** plus a **domain-adversarial loss** to make the discriminator dynamics-invariant. The "adaptive" is adaptation to dynamics, not to demonstration quality.
- **Portability:** Tangential to noisy references, but **genuinely relevant if your noise is a sim-to-real / embodiment mismatch** rather than sensor noise — i.e. if the reference is "correct" but dynamically infeasible for your humanoid. In that case the dynamics-invariant discriminator idea and §11's constrained-style work are the right family.

### Confidence-Conditioned Value Functions for Offline RL
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04607 — Hong, Kumar, Levine. ICLR 2023. No code found.
- **Mechanism:** Standard conservative offline RL bakes in one fixed pessimism level. This learns $Q$ **as a function of the confidence level** via a modified Bellman backup, so a single network represents the whole family of conservative estimates simultaneously, with a guarantee that the estimate is conservative at any requested confidence. At evaluation the agent *adapts* its confidence level based on observed history instead of committing at train time.
- **Portability:** Medium. The reusable idea is **conditioning the policy on a scalar trust level and choosing it at deployment** — e.g. condition your tracking policy on "how much do I trust this reference," train across the full range, and pick per-clip at test time. This is the same move Decision Transformer makes with return, applied to reliability.

## 8. Offline RL / filtering with mixed-quality data

### BAIL — Best-Action Imitation Learning for Batch Deep RL
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12179
- Code: https://github.com/lanyavik/BAIL (community implementation; includes pre-generated batch datasets)
- NeurIPS 2020. Chen, Zhou, Wang, Wu, Ross.
- **Mechanism:** Three stages, no Q-learning at all. (1) Fit an **upper envelope** $V_\phi$ over Monte-Carlo returns: $\min_\phi\sum_i[V_\phi(s_i)-G_i]^2+\lambda\|w\|^2$ s.t. $V_\phi(s_i)\ge G_i$, relaxed in practice to a penalized loss $L^K(\phi)=\sum_i(V_\phi(s_i)-G_i)^2\{\mathbb{1}_{V\ge G}+K\cdot\mathbb{1}_{V<G}\}+\lambda\|w\|^2$ with $K=1000$ — i.e. hugely asymmetric penalty so $V$ traces the *top* of the return cloud rather than its mean. (2) **Select** pairs satisfying $G_i > x\,V_\phi(s_i)$, with $x$ calibrated so exactly $p\%$ survive ($p=25$). (3) Vanilla BC on the survivors. Beats batch Q-learning and is much faster.
- **Portability:** **High and cheap.** The upper-envelope trick generalizes to your setting as a **state-conditional achievability threshold**: fit an asymmetric-penalty regressor to "best achievable tracking quality at this state" and drop/downweight reference frames whose achieved quality falls below $x\cdot V(s)$. This is percentile filtering made state-aware, which plain 10%-BC is not.

### CRR — Critic Regularized Regression
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15134
- Code: in Acme — https://github.com/google-deepmind/acme/tree/master/acme/agents/jax/crr
- NeurIPS 2020. Wang, Novikov, Zolna, Springenberg, Reed, Shahriari, Siegel, Merel, Gulcehre, Heess, de Freitas.
- **Mechanism:** Weighted BC with a critic-derived filter: $\arg\max_\pi\mathbb{E}_{(s,a)\sim B}[f(Q_\theta,\pi,s,a)\log\pi(a|s)]$. Two filter variants: **CRR binary max**, $f=\mathbb{1}[\hat A_\theta(s,a)>0]$ (hard filter), and **CRR exp**, $f=\exp(\hat A_\theta(s,a)/\beta)$ (soft, temperature $\beta$). Advantage is estimated by sampling $m=4$ actions from the current policy: $A_{\text{mean}}$ subtracts the mean of those $Q$'s, $A_{\max}$ the max (pessimistic, pairs with the binary filter). Scales to high-dim state/action.
- **Portability:** **Very high — arguably the most directly implementable idea in this whole report.** You already have a dense reward and can train a critic. Weighting your tracking/BC term by $\exp(\hat A(s,a)/\beta)$ or $\mathbb{1}[\hat A>0]$ automatically suppresses reference frames whose prescribed motion is worse than what the policy would do itself — which is precisely "ignore the noisy part of the reference." No confidence labels, no discriminator, no second dataset.

### Percentile / filtered BC
- The `10%BC` / `%BC` baselines are standard in D4RL-era offline RL: sort trajectories by return, keep the top $p\%$, run BC. Two anchor references: **When Should We Prefer Offline RL Over Behavioral Cloning?** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05618 (Kumar, Hong, Singh, Levine, ICLR 2022), which shows offline RL beats BC precisely under **sparse rewards or noisy data sources**, and that policies trained on *sufficiently noisy suboptimal* data can beat BC-on-expert-data on long-horizon problems; and **Should We Ever Prefer Decision Transformer for Offline RL?** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10174 (2025), which re-litigates %BC vs. DT.
- **Portability:** Trivially portable as a **baseline you should run first** — filter reference clips by achieved tracking return, keep the top $p\%$. Kumar et al.'s finding is a warning though: naive top-$p$ filtering throws away the long-horizon-useful noisy data.

### Decision Transformer — RL via Sequence Modeling
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01345 — Code: https://github.com/kzl/decision-transformer — NeurIPS 2021. Chen, Lu, Rajeswaran, Lee, Grover, Laskin, Abbeel, Srinivas, Mordatch.
- **Mechanism:** Causal transformer over $(\hat R_t, s_t, a_t)$ triples trained with plain autoregressive action prediction. The desired **return-to-go is an input conditioning token**, so suboptimal data isn't filtered — it's *labeled* by its own return and learned from, and you ask for high return at test time.
- **Portability:** Medium. The reusable primitive is **conditioning on a quality token instead of filtering by it**: train the tracking policy conditioned on a per-clip (or per-window) reference-quality scalar, then request "high quality" at deployment. You keep all the data, including the noisy clips, which matters if your clean set is small.

### RvS — What is Essential for Offline RL via Supervised Learning?
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10751 — Code: https://github.com/scottemmons/rvs — ICLR 2022. Emmons, Eysenbach, Kostrikov, Levine.
- **Mechanism:** Ablates DT down to essentials: a **two-layer MLP maximizing likelihood, conditioned on goal or reward**, matches far more complex methods. The two things that actually matter are **model capacity** and **what you condition on**; the transformer and the TD learning are largely not the point.
- **Portability:** High as a **cost-control finding** — if you go the conditioning route, you don't need a transformer. Condition an MLP on a scalar reference-quality/goal signal and size it properly.

## 9. Noise-robust behavior cloning and robust losses

### BCND — Behavioral Cloning from Noisy Demonstrations
- **No arXiv version.** OpenReview: https://openreview.net/forum?id=zrT3HcsWSAt (currently behind a bot check)
- ICLR 2021. Sasaki & Yamashina. No official code found.
- **Mechanism:** The central claim, as characterized by the follow-up literature (Wang et al., ICML 2024 explicitly summarize it this way): **the agent's own policy can serve as the confidence estimate**. BC is run iteratively with the *previous* policy's action probability as the per-sample weight, so samples the current policy already finds plausible dominate and outlier/noisy actions self-suppress; an ensemble is used to prevent collapse onto a degenerate mode. Both DP-IL and other follow-ups note the practical caveat: **it can be unstable and hard to converge.**
- **Portability:** High-concept, with a caveat. "Weight each demo sample by your own current policy's likelihood of it" needs no labels and no second dataset — the analog is weighting the tracking term by how plausible the reference frame is under the current policy's own predicted next state. But heed the reported instability: it's a positive-feedback loop, so it needs an ensemble or a trust-region / floor on the weights.

### Symmetric and robust losses
- The IL-specific treatment is **RIL-Co** (§6): symmetric loss condition $\ell(z)+\ell(-z)=c$, AP loss preferred over unhinged.
- **Robust Imitation Learning from Corrupted Demonstrations** (§6): Median-of-Means.
- **Adaptive t-Momentum-based Optimization for Unknown Ratio of Outliers in Amateur Data in Imitation Learning** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00625 — IROS 2021, Ilboudo, Kobayashi, Sugimoto. No code found. Builds the **Student's-t distribution** into the *optimizer* (a t-momentum Adam variant): heavy-tailed modeling downweights outlying gradient contributions, and the adaptive version does so **without knowing the outlier ratio in advance**. Validated on two manipulation tasks with imperfect demos.
- **Portability of this cluster: the highest effort-to-payoff ratio in the report.** Three independent, orthogonal, near-free changes to a dense tracking loss: (1) replace MSE with a **bounded** per-frame error (Huber, or better, $1-\exp(-\|e\|^2/\sigma^2)$ which is already bounded and symmetric-ish — note most motion-tracking rewards *already* use $\exp(-\|e\|^2)$, so you may be partway there); (2) MoM aggregation across minibatch groups; (3) a t-momentum optimizer. None requires a second dataset or a confidence model. Caveat worth stating in the parent report: the GCE / symmetric-cross-entropy literature (Zhang & Sabuncu 2018; Ghosh et al. 2017) is **classification-specific** — the $\ell(z)+\ell(-z)=c$ symmetry condition doesn't transfer to a continuous regression target, so for a tracking reward the right analogs are bounded/redescending M-estimators (Huber, Tukey, Welsch/Geman-McClure), not GCE.

### Positive-unlabeled learning in IL
- **PURL — Positive-Unlabeled Reward Learning** — https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00459 — Xu & Denil, 2019 (CoRL 2020). No official code found. Formally connects GAIL-style adversarial reward learning and supervised reward regression to **PU learning**, then applies a large-scale PU algorithm to fix reward **under-estimation and over-estimation simultaneously** — the discriminator otherwise treats the unlabeled agent data as pure negatives even when some of it is expert-quality.
- Also PU-based: **DWBC** (§4), **UID** (§2), **ROIDA** (§10).
- **Portability:** Medium-high. If you hand-label a small set of clean reference clips and leave the rest unlabeled, the PU class-prior correction ($\eta$ in DWBC's Eq. 4) is the statistically correct way to avoid treating the unlabeled remainder as all-bad.

## 10. 2023–2026 works on noisy demonstrations

Ordered roughly by relevance to your setting.

### Constrained Style Learning from Imperfect Demonstrations under Task Optimality ⭐
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09371 — **CoRL 2025.** Wen, Li, He, **Hutter** (ETH RSL). No code link found on the arXiv page.
- **Mechanism:** Legged robots, and the closest paper in this entire report to your problem. Formulates style-imitation-from-imperfect-demos as a **CMDP**: maximize the style/imitation objective *subject to a constraint that task performance stays near-optimal*, enforced by an **adaptively adjusted Lagrange multiplier**. That multiplier is the mechanism: when the demonstration would cost task performance, $\lambda$ automatically suppresses the imitation term; where the demo is helpful, it lets it through. Validated on ANYmal-D hardware — 14.5% mechanical-energy reduction and improved gait agility.
- **Portability:** **Direct.** This is the same problem with a different label. "Track the reference, but only to the extent it doesn't wreck the task" is precisely what a Lagrangian on the tracking term buys you, and it's cheap to bolt onto an existing PPO tracking setup.

### Task-Centric Policy Optimization from Misaligned Motion Priors (TCMP)
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19411 — ⚠️ **WITHDRAWN (v2, Feb 2026)**, authors' note: "Work requires further details and not complete yet." Zheng, Feng, Nie, Qin. Humanoid.
- **Mechanism:** Treats imitation as a **conditional regularizer rather than a co-equal objective** — dynamically suppresses the imitation gradient when the motion prior conflicts with task progress, and preserves geometric awareness of feasible descent directions.
- **Portability:** The framing is right and matches the CoRL 2025 paper above; **cite the idea, don't cite the paper** — it's withdrawn and no PDF is available.

### SUGAR — Scalable Human-Video-Driven Generalizable Humanoid Loco-Manipulation
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20373 — Project: https://tianshuwu.github.io/sugar-humanoid/ — May 2026. Wu, Kong, Chen, Yu, Ye, Li, Wang, Dong (PKU).
- **Mechanism:** Explicitly names your failure mode: motion priors from human video are "inherently imperfect, suffering from occlusion, contact artifacts, and **retargeting errors** that render them unsuitable for direct policy learning." Stage 2 is a **privileged physics-based refiner** that converts imperfect priors into physically feasible skills using a **unified mimic reward + progressive state pool** — i.e. repair the reference in a privileged sim pass before policy learning, rather than trying to make the tracking policy absorb the noise.
- **Portability:** **Direct.** "Refine the reference first with a privileged policy, then train the deployable policy on the refined reference" is a strong architectural alternative to reward-weighting, and it's the one the recent humanoid literature is converging on.

### Restoring Noisy Demonstration for Imitation Learning With Diffusion Models
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14467 — **IEEE TNNLS**, Sept 2025. Chen, Yong, Sun (NTU). No code found.
- **Mechanism:** Two-stage **filter-then-restore**: first filter clean samples out of the demonstration set, then train **conditional diffusion models on the clean subset to reconstruct the noisy ones** — so noisy demos are repaired rather than discarded. Evaluated on robot-arm manipulation, dexterous hands, and locomotion; ablations across noise types and levels.
- **Portability:** **High.** Reference trajectories are exactly the kind of smooth, structured signal a diffusion model can denoise, and "restore rather than discard" preserves data you can't afford to throw away.

### DP-IL — Imitation Learning from Purified Demonstrations
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07143 — Code: https://github.com/yunke-wang/dp-il — **ICML 2024.** Wang, Dong, Zhao, Du, Xu.
- **Mechanism:** Counterintuitive and elegant: **add more noise, then denoise**. Run a forward diffusion process on the imperfect demos to step $t^*$ (this smooths the *existing* noise into the tractable Gaussian noise the model knows how to remove), then run the reverse generative process to recover near-optimal demonstrations. Theorem 2 bounds the $L_2$ distance between purified and optimal demonstration. Downstream BC/GAIL then runs on purified data. MuJoCo + RoboSuite.
- **Portability:** **High**, and it pairs naturally with §1's noise-injection ideas — you already have a noise model for your references, so the forward process is well-specified.

### SMILE — Good Better Best: Self-Motivated Imitation Learning for Noisy Demonstrations
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15815 — Yuan, Li, Heng, Zhang, Wang, Oct 2023. No code found.
- **Mechanism:** Uses **diffusion timesteps as a metric of expertise gap**. Trains a conditioned Q-function/noise predictor such that $t(\tau)=\arg\max_{t'} Q_{\theta,t'}(a|a^{\pi_\phi},s)$ estimates how many diffusion steps separate the demonstrator's policy from the agent's current policy — proved equivalent to their expertise gap. When $t(\tau)=0$, the demonstrator is no better than the agent, so the demo is **automatically filtered out**. Fully self-supervised; the filter ratchets as the agent improves.
- **Portability:** Medium-high — "drop the reference once you already track better than it" is a clean self-motivated curriculum, though it assumes you can compare policy-to-reference quality, which you can via tracking return.

### Counterfactual Behavior Cloning (Counter-BC)
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10760 — Sagheb & Losey (Virginia Tech), May 2025 (rev. Aug 2026). Videos linked; no code repo found.
- **Mechanism:** Premise: "all of the human's demonstrations are trying to convey an underlying policy, while the noise and sub-optimality within their behaviors obfuscates the data." So **expand the dataset with nearby counterfactual actions the human could have taken**, autonomously modify demonstrations within that region during training, and extract the *simplest* policy consistent with the expanded region — trading exact imitation for complexity reduction. Proofs for recovery under noise, multiple users, and varying skill.
- **Portability:** Medium-high — "treat the reference as a *region* of acceptable behavior rather than a point target" is a reframe with real consequences for a tracking reward: widen the tolerance tube instead of chasing a noisy point.

### ILID — How to Leverage Diverse Demonstrations in Offline Imitation Learning
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17476 — Code: https://github.com/HansenHua/ILID-ICML24 — **ICML 2024.** Yue, Liu, Hua, Ren, Lin, Zhang, Zhang.
- **Mechanism:** Argues that selecting data by state-action similarity to expert demos discards useful *diverse* behavior. Instead selects by **resultant state**: train a state-only discriminator, keep actions whose *successor* states land on the expert state manifold, then roll back to recover the causal state-actions that led there. Lightweight weighted BC on top. SOTA on 20/21 benchmarks, typically 2–5×, at BC-level runtime.
- **Portability:** **High.** "Judge an action by where it lands, not by whether it matches the reference action" is exactly the right criterion when the reference *actions* are noisy but the reference *states* are roughly right — the common case in retargeted motion.

### ROIDA — Robust Offline Imitation Learning from Diverse Auxiliary Data
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03626 — Code: https://github.com/uditaghosh/roida — **TMLR 2025.** Ghosh, Raychaudhuri, Li, Karydis, Roy-Chowdhury.
- **Mechanism:** Hybrid that refuses to choose between filtering and using bad data. A PU-learning discriminator yields $\tilde r(s,a)=\log\frac{d(s,a)}{1-d(s,a)}$; transitions above threshold $\tau$ go into **weighted BC**, while *low*-quality samples are still exploited via **TD learning** on the learned reward to steer the policy toward high-reward states and improve long-horizon return. No assumptions about the quality mix.
- **Portability:** **High**, and it's the right answer to the Kumar et al. warning in §8: high-confidence reference frames supervise directly, low-confidence ones still contribute through value bootstrapping instead of being thrown away.

### ADR — Imitating from auxiliary imperfect demonstrations via Adversarial Density Weighted Regression
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20351 — Zhang, Zhuang, Xu, Yang, Huang, Wang, Zhang. May 2024, rev. Jan 2025. Code promised in abstract; repo not confirmed.
- **Mechanism:** One-step supervised framework with **no Bellman operator** — avoids the cumulative sub-optimality offset that multi-step reward-based updates accrue, and avoids needing a hard-to-tune conservatism term for OOD state-actions. Integrates a one-step **density-weighted BC** objective. +89.5% over IQL with ground-truth rewards on Adroit and Kitchen.
- **Portability:** Medium — the density-weighting form is reusable; the "avoid Bellman" motivation is an offline-RL concern you don't have online.

### Robust Offline IL Through State-level Trajectory Stitching
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22524 — Wang, Mei, Cao, Yuan, Wang, Sun, Chen. Mar 2025, rev. Sep 2025. No code found.
- **Mechanism:** State-based search that **stitches state-action pairs across imperfect demonstrations** into new, more diverse training trajectories, exploiting environment dynamics rather than filtering.
- **Portability:** Medium — stitching clean segments across noisy reference clips is a plausible data-augmentation strategy for motion libraries.

### TREND — Tri-teaching for Robust Preference-based RL with Demonstrations
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06079 — Project: https://shuaiyihuang.github.io/publications/TREND — **ICRA 2025.** Huang, Levy, Gupta, Ekpo, Zheng, Shrivastava (UMD).
- **Mechanism:** Trains **three** reward models; each identifies the preference pairs on which it has *small loss* (the small-loss criterion for clean-sample identification) and hands those to its peers for their updates. Needs only 1–3 expert demos and holds ~90% success on robot manipulation at **40% preference-noise**.
- **Portability:** Medium-high. The **small-loss criterion** — "samples your model already fits well are probably the clean ones" — is a one-line heuristic that works surprisingly well, and it's the same family as RIL-Co's co-pseudo-labeling with an extra network for stability.

### GR-RL — Going Dexterous and Precise for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01801 — Li et al. (20+ authors), Dec 2025. No code link found.
- **Mechanism:** Opens by stating "in highly dexterous and precise manipulation tasks, **human demonstrations are noisy and suboptimal**." Stage 1 filters demonstrations using **offline RL with sparse rewards, treating the learned Q-value as a progress function** and retaining only trajectories that positively contribute to task completion. Then morphological-symmetry augmentation, then online RL refinement with a latent-space noise predictor. 83.3% success on autonomous shoe-lacing.
- **Portability:** **High.** "Use a Q-value as a progress function to filter demonstrations" is a production-grade instantiation of CRR-style advantage filtering, from a large-scale robotics effort — evidence the CRR recommendation in §8 is the one that survives contact with real systems.

### Others worth a line
- **CIQL** (§2) — RA-L 2024, transition-level confidence, code at https://github.com/XizoB/CIQL.
- **SSDF — Learning from Imperfect Demonstrations with Self-Supervision for Robotic Manipulation** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08957 — **ICRA 2025.** Wu et al. Self-supervised data filtering that scores *segments of failed trajectories* by quality and recycles the good parts into the training set. No code found.
- **EIL — Extraneousness-Aware Imitation Learning** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01379 — ICRA 2023, Zheng, Hu, Yuan, Chen, Xu. Targets *task-irrelevant-but-coherent* segments ("wiping sweat while cutting potatoes") rather than random noise: learns action-conditioned embeddings, retrieves task-relevant observations across demos, excludes extraneous subsequences. Matches perfect-demo performance. No code URL found on the arXiv page.
- **NAC — Reinforcement Learning from Imperfect Demonstrations** — https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05313 — Gao, Xu, Lin, Yu, Levine, Darrell, 2018. Historically important and structurally relevant: unlike DQfD, which adds a *supervised* margin loss forcing the agent to mimic every demo, **Normalized Actor-Critic uses the same objective for demonstrations and for interactive refinement**, normalizing the Q-function to suppress unseen actions. Because there's no separate imitation loss, it is **not forced to mimic all examples** — which is exactly the property you want from a tracking objective that must tolerate bad reference frames.
- **Learning from Imperfect Demonstrations via Adversarial Confidence Transfer** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02967 — ICRA 2022.
- **LOPAL — Local Performance-Aware Active Learning from Imperfect Demonstrations** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16888 — IEEE RA-L, 2026.

## 12. Synthesis — what actually transfers to "RL tracking a noisy reference with a dense reward"

Sorted by (portability × implementation cost). The structural mismatch worth flagging up front: **most of this literature spends its machinery on inferring a reward you don't need.** T-REX/D-REX/SSRR/Bayesian REX, the whole GAIL-confidence family, and the DICE family all exist because there is no reward signal. You have one. So the reward-learning half is dead weight; what you want is the **weighting, confidence-estimation, and filtering half** — which in most of these papers is a small, separable component.

**Tier 1 — do these first, near-zero cost, no new data or labels:**
1. **CRR-style advantage weighting** on the tracking term: $f=\exp(\hat A(s,a)/\beta)$ or $\mathbb{1}[\hat A>0]$. You already train a critic. Automatically suppresses reference frames the policy can beat. GR-RL (2025) validates this at scale as a "Q-as-progress-function" filter.
2. **Bounded/robust per-frame error** + **Median-of-Means** aggregation across minibatch groups. Caps the influence of any single badly-retargeted frame. Note the GCE caveat in §9: use redescending M-estimators, not classification losses.
3. **Lagrangian-constrained imitation** (Constrained Style Learning, CoRL 2025): make the tracking term a constraint with an adaptive multiplier rather than a co-equal reward term.

**Tier 2 — moderate effort, high payoff:**
4. **ILEED-style state-dependent expertise** $\rho_\varphi(s,\omega_i)$ as a per-frame inverse-variance weight, per reference clip. Unsupervised, joint MLE, and its continuous-action form is *already* a precision weight on a squared error.
5. **D-REX / SSRR noise-injection calibration**: inject graded, *structured* (S3RR) noise into references, fit the degradation curve, use it to calibrate confidence without labels.
6. **ILID resultant-state criterion**: judge reference frames by where they land, not by action match — the right criterion when reference states are roughly right but reference actions are noisy.
7. **ROIDA's hybrid**: high-confidence frames supervise directly, low-confidence frames still contribute through TD bootstrapping rather than being discarded.

**Tier 3 — architectural bets:**
8. **Refine-the-reference-first** (SUGAR's privileged physics refiner; diffusion restoration in 2510.14467 and DP-IL). Fix the reference in a privileged pass, then train the deployable policy on clean data. This is where the 2025–2026 humanoid literature is converging.
9. **CAIL's bilevel meta-gradient**: learn per-sample confidence by backpropagating held-out rollout return through the policy update. You have a better outer signal than CAIL did (real task return vs. their ranking proxy), so this is *more* applicable to you than to the original paper.
10. **Quality-conditioned policy** (DT/RvS/confidence-conditioned): condition on a reference-quality scalar and request "high quality" at deployment, instead of filtering. Keeps all data; RvS says an MLP suffices.

**Explicitly low-portability:** OptiDICE, DemoDICE, LobsDICE's dual machinery (offline-specific); Bayesian REX's MCMC (safety-oriented); ADAIL (dynamics adaptation, not demo quality — unless your "noise" is really embodiment infeasibility, in which case it becomes relevant).
